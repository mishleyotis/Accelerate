## Slide 5: Industry Forces

**Headline:** A generational wealth transfer, PE-fueled consolidation, and margin pressure are redefining who survives in wealth management

**Sub-vertical label:** WEALTH MANAGEMENT RIAs IN 2026

**Introduction:** Here is what the data says about where the industry is heading.

**Key Statistics:**

### Stat 1: $105T

- in wealth transferring to heirs by 2048, and 81% plan to fire their parent's advisor
- The largest intergenerational wealth transfer in history is underway. Firms that haven't built relationships with the next generation will lose the majority of inherited assets.

### Stat 2: 209

- RIA M&A deals closed through September 2025, up 34% year over year
- PE-backed firms now control 23% of all $100M+ RIA assets despite being fewer than 4% of firms by count. Consolidation is structural, not cyclical.

### Stat 3: 18%

- operating margins for smaller RIAs, a record low
- Advisory expenses have reached 82% of revenue for sub-$1B firms. Without technology-driven efficiency, revenue growth alone cannot close the gap.

## Slide 7: Pain Points

**Question:** Do these pain points sound familiar?

**Subtitle:** Manual workflows and fragmented data are limiting the growth your advisors can deliver

**Pain Points:**

**"Our clients expect a digital experience we can't deliver."**
- 78% of clients expect interactive digital experiences from their advisor. 92% would consider switching for poor technology, and 44% already have.

**"Our advisors spend more time on admin than on clients."**
- The average advisor spends 40% of their time on administrative tasks. AI-enabled firms are saving 10+ hours per week per advisor, enough capacity to serve 20 additional clients annually.

**"We're about to lose our clients' children."**
- 81% of heirs plan to leave their parent's advisor. 63% of Gen Z and 54% of millennials would switch for integrated estate planning. The transfer window is open now.

**"Our client data is scattered across custodians that don't talk to each other."**
- Multi-custodian data fragmentation is the number one barrier advisors cite in technology selection. Without a unified household view, AI initiatives stall and personalization stays generic.
