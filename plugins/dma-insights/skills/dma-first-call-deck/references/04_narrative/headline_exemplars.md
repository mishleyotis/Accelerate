## Section B: 20 Gold-Standard Headline Patterns by Slide Type

### Title Slides (3 patterns)

**Pattern 1: Big Idea Statement**
- Structure: "[Client]'s [initiative] [action verb] [specific outcome] by [timeframe]"
- Example: "Pacific Coast CU's digital transformation closes the 0.7 maturity gap by Q4 2026"
- When to use: Opening slide, reengagement moments, phase transitions
- Word count: 12–18 words
- Power: Combines WHO (client), WHAT (initiative), HOW (action verb), WHERE (outcome), WHEN (timeframe)

**Pattern 2: Provocative Question**
- Structure: "What would [outcome] mean for [client]'s [metric]?"
- Example: "What would reaching peer median maturity mean for Pacific Coast's member retention?"
- When to use: When you want the audience to sit forward; works after a data reveal
- Word count: 11–16 words
- Power: Shifts from assertion to co-creation; invites mental commitment

**Pattern 3: Scope + Stakes**
- Structure: "[Initiative] for [Client] — [N deliverables] addressing [core challenge]"
- Example: "Digital Maturity Assessment for Pacific Coast CU — 136 capabilities revealing the path from 2.4 to 3.2"
- When to use: Executive summary covers; when engagement scope matters to the audience
- Word count: 14–22 words
- Power: Establishes both breadth and focus in one breath

---

### Data/Summary Slides (4 patterns)

**Pattern 4: Score + Context**
- Structure: "[Client] scores [X]/5, [positioning] relative to [benchmark]"
- Example: "Pacific Coast scores 2.4/5 on digital maturity — 0.2 points below peer median, but concentrated weakness in just two pillars"
- When to use: Introducing assessment results; benchmark comparisons
- Word count: 15–20 words
- Power: Immediate calibration; positions client in competitive context without judgment

**Pattern 5: Paradox Reveal**
- Structure: "[Strength] at [score] masks [weakness] at [score] — the net effect is [impact]"
- Example: "Sales automation scores 3.8/5 (top quartile) but process standardization scores 1.9/5 — creating false efficiency that scales inconsistently"
- When to use: When a strength hides a critical problem; prevents overconfidence
- Word count: 18–25 words
- Power: Forces audiences to see beyond the headline number

**Pattern 6: Trend + Implication**
- Structure: "[Metric] [direction] [amount] since [date], [creating/threatening] [consequence]"
- Example: "Digital capabilities adoption increased 40% since Q1 2025, but infrastructure maturity lags by 2 generations — creating scaling risk"
- When to use: Time-series data; momentum conversations; bottleneck framing
- Word count: 16–22 words
- Power: Connects observation to risk or opportunity

**Pattern 7: Distribution Insight**
- Structure: "[N] of [total] capabilities cluster [below/above] benchmark, concentrated in [area]"
- Example: "76 of 136 capabilities cluster below peer benchmark, concentrated in AI/ML readiness and data governance — two areas with known resource constraints"
- When to use: Segmenting a large capability universe; showing where help is needed most
- Word count: 18–24 words
- Power: Converts overwhelming data into actionable geography

---

### Gap/Opportunity Slides (4 patterns)

**Pattern 8: Top-N Impact**
- Structure: "[N] capabilities close [X]% of the gap — [list them]"
- Example: "5 capabilities close 67% of the gap to peer median: Data governance, process automation, change management, cloud migration, and AI governance"
- When to use: Prioritization slides; showing the Pareto dynamic
- Word count: 14–18 words
- Power: Proves that transformation doesn't require fixing everything simultaneously

**Pattern 9: Before/After**
- Structure: "[Current state] costs [X]; [proposed state] saves [Y] annually, or enables [outcome]"
- Example: "Manual processes cost 15 FTEs annually; cloud-based automation reduces this to 4 FTEs and redeployws capacity to strategic work"
- When to use: ROI conversations; resource reallocation pitches
- Word count: 15–20 words
- Power: Converts capability into business value

**Pattern 10: Hidden Opportunity**
- Structure: "While [visible issue] draws attention, [hidden factor] represents [X]x more impact"
- Example: "While process standardization dominates conversation, upgrading data governance unlocks 3x more capability benefit through improved decision velocity"
- When to use: Course-correction slides; countering conventional wisdom
- Word count: 17–22 words
- Power: Repositions priority; creates differentiation through insight

**Pattern 11: Competitive Pressure**
- Structure: "[N]% of peers have already [action] — [client] closing this gap [prevents/enables] [outcome]"
- Example: "87% of peer FIs have deployed advanced analytics; Pacific Coast closing this gap prevents margin compression from competitive intelligence disadvantage"
- When to use: Urgency slides; benchmarking moments
- Word count: 16–21 words
- Power: Combines FOMO with business consequence

---

### Recommendation/Roadmap Slides (3 patterns)

**Pattern 12: Phased Progression**
- Structure: "A [N]-phase roadmap moves [client] from [current] to [target] — starting with [quick win]"
- Example: "A 3-phase roadmap moves Pacific Coast from 2.4 to 3.5 maturity — starting with data governance consolidation (12 weeks, enables downstream work)"
- When to use: Roadmap overview; sequencing conversations
- Word count: 18–24 words
- Power: Establishes both destination and first step; reduces overwhelm

**Pattern 13: Investment → Return**
- Structure: "[Investment] in [capability] yields [X]x return through [mechanism] within [timeframe]"
- Example: "$2.5M investment in cloud infrastructure yields 3.2x return within 18 months through operational cost savings and accelerated capability deployment"
- When to use: Budget defense; business case conversations
- Word count: 16–20 words
- Power: Ties spending to measurable outcome

**Pattern 14: Dependency Chain**
- Structure: "[Phase 1] enables [Phase 2] which unlocks [ultimate outcome] — sequence matters"
- Example: "Data consolidation (Phase 1) enables process automation (Phase 2) which unlocks AI/ML deployment (Phase 3) — sequence matters because each phase requires the prior one"
- When to use: Explaining why sequencing is non-negotiable
- Word count: 20–26 words
- Power: Prevents clients from attempting to parallelize sequenced work

---

### Close/Next-Steps Slides (3 patterns)

**Pattern 15: Deadline + Consequence**
- Structure: "Approving by [date] enables [specific outcome]; delay past [date] risks [consequence]"
- Example: "Approving by April 15 enables Phase 1 kickoff in May and completion by Q3; delay past May 1 pushes completion to Q4 and risks losing vendor availability"
- When to use: Decision slides; creating urgency without aggression
- Word count: 18–24 words
- Power: Connects approval to outcome; makes cost of delay visible

**Pattern 16: Mobilization**
- Structure: "[N] actions over [timeframe] position [client] for [outcome] — [first action] starts [date]"
- Example: "4 actions over 30 days position Pacific Coast for Phase 1 launch: steering committee kickoff (April 8), stakeholder interviews (April 10–15), requirements definition (April 17–25), and vendor selection (April 28)"
- When to use: Transition from strategy to execution
- Word count: 20–28 words
- Power: Converts abstract commitment into calendar-based accountability

**Pattern 17: Vision Anchor**
- Structure: "[Client] can be [aspirational state] by [date] — today's decision is the first step"
- Example: "Pacific Coast can be a top-quartile digital bank by end of 2026 — today's approval unlocks that path"
- When to use: Closing remarks; emotional resonance moment
- Word count: 14–18 words
- Power: Reminds audience of the "why" behind the "what"

---

### Section Break Slides (3 patterns)

**Pattern 18: Transition Claim**
- Structure: "From [previous topic insight] to [next topic promise]"
- Example: "From understanding where you are (maturity assessment) to defining how you'll move (roadmap and execution plan)"
- When to use: Between logical sections; maintaining narrative flow
- Word count: 12–16 words
- Power: Prevents audience disorientation; signals progress

**Pattern 19: Question Frame**
- Structure: "Given [established fact], how should [client] approach [next topic]?"
- Example: "Given that 76 capabilities lag peer benchmark, how should Pacific Coast sequence its investment to maximize near-term wins?"
- When to use: Transitioning from problem to solution
- Word count: 14–18 words
- Power: Frames next section as answering a logical question

**Pattern 20: Progress Marker**
- Structure: "[What we've established] → [What we'll explore next]"
- Example: "You're at 2.4/5 maturity → Here's how to reach 3.5/5 in 18 months"
- When to use: Mid-deck recalibration; showing progress through the story
- Word count: 10–14 words
- Power: Conciseness and clarity; shows forward motion

## Section C: Headline Construction Discipline

### The Three Tests

**Test 1: The Claim Test**
Does the headline make a specific claim, or is it just a label?
- Label (bad): "Digital Maturity Assessment Results"
- Claim (good): "Pacific Coast scores 2.4/5 — 0.2 points below peer median in a landscape where digital maturity drives member acquisition"

**Test 2: The Consequence Test**
Does the headline explain why this slide matters to the audience?
- No consequence: "Process Standardization: 45%"
- With consequence: "Process standardization at 45% explains why your regional teams follow 7 different approval workflows — and why standardization is your biggest quick win"

**Test 3: The Specificity Test**
Could the headline apply to any client, or is it customized to this one?
- Generic: "Roadmap Overview"
- Specific: "Pacific Coast's 3-phase roadmap: data consolidation (12 weeks) → process automation (16 weeks) → AI deployment (12 weeks)"

### Anti-Patterns to Avoid

1. **The Placeholder Label**: "Overview", "Executive Summary", "Key Findings" — these add no information
2. **The Vague Assertion**: "Significant opportunity exists" — significant by what measure?
3. **The Buried Lead**: "Review of Capability Assessment Results and Benchmark Comparison Analysis" — get to the point in 8–12 words
4. **The Question Without Stakes**: "Are we ready for digital transformation?" — should be "Here's why current readiness level blocks our Q3 target"
5. **The Jargon Fog**: "Operationalize synergistic digital-first capabilities" — unclear what you're actually recommending

### Quick Editing Checklist

For any headline, ask:
- Does it contain a number, name, or timeframe? (Specificity check)
- Does it explain an insight or consequence, not just a topic? (Claim check)
- Can it be cut to 8–12 words without losing meaning? (Precision check)
- Would a client nod and say "that's why we need this" after hearing it? (Relevance check)
- Is it in active voice with a strong verb? (Energy check)

## Section F: Headline Quality Standards by Audience Type

### C-Suite Audiences
Expect: Outcome + timeline + investment
- Example: "An 18-month, $6M digital transformation lifts member retention by 12% and saves $2.4M annually in operational costs"
- Avoid: Process details, capability names, technical jargon

### Department Head Audiences
Expect: Outcome + impact to their team + involvement level
- Example: "Automating claims triage reduces your team's processing time by 40% and redeployes 3 FTEs to strategy work"
- Avoid: Enterprise metrics they don't own, technology choices

### Technical Teams
Expect: Architecture clarity + integration sequencing + effort estimate
- Example: "Data consolidation comes first (16 weeks, requires parallel system run); process automation follows (12 weeks, depends on consolidated data); sequence protects infrastructure"
- Avoid: Business case alone, ROI messaging without technical detail

## Canonical Headline Patterns (from Alliant Reference Deck)

These are the MANDATORY headline templates. The LLM must follow the structure, only swapping client-specific data.

### Slide 1 — Title Cover (max 80 chars at 24pt)
**Pattern:** `Your digital maturity blueprint: where [Client] stands today and the investments that accelerate what's next`
**Alliant gold standard:** "Your digital maturity blueprint: where Alliant Insurance stands today and the investments that accelerate what's next"
**Anti-pattern:** "Financial Partners Credit Union: from 212 tools to platform clarity" ← too short, colon-stat format, no narrative arc

### Slide 6 — Org Profile (Sh2 headline, max ~130 chars at ~26pt, 2 lines — 40-shape layout)
**Pattern:** `[Client]'s [anchor metric] [differentiator] create(s) a foundation for [transformation outcome]`
**Alliant:** "Alliant's $5.1 billion specialty-focused platform and 21.9% organic growth create a foundation for enterprise digital acceleration"
**Anti-pattern:** "Financial Partners' $2.5B foundation for digital growth" ← drops differentiator clause and outcome
**Note:** Old 7-shape layout used Sh1 at 17pt/125ch. New 40-shape layout uses Sh2 at ~26pt/~130ch over 2 lines.

### Slide 9 — DMA Summary (max 100 chars at 17pt)
**Pattern:** `[Client] scores [X]/5 on digital maturity — [peer position], with [strongest pillar] leading and [weakest pillar] presenting the greatest [outcome] opportunity`
**Alliant:** "Alliant scores 3.05 out of 5 on digital maturity — above the peer median of 2.80, with operations leading and client experience presenting the greatest growth opportunity"
**Anti-pattern:** "Where Financial Partners Credit Union stands and what comes next" ← zero data, completely generic

### Slide 13 — Assessment + Radar (max ~130 chars at 20pt)
**Pattern:** `[Strongest pillar] anchors the scorecard at [level] maturity, while [weakest pillar] and [second weakest] present the greatest opportunities to close peer gaps`
**Alliant:** "Operations excellence anchors the scorecard at Advanced maturity, while client experience and governance present the greatest opportunities to close peer gaps"

### Slide 14 — Heatmap (max 124 chars at 21pt)
**Pattern:** `[Top capability] leads at [score], while [weakest 1] ([score]) and [weakest 2] ([score]) present the highest-impact opportunities for transformation`
**Alliant:** "Cybersecurity leads at 4.43, while client experience (2.37) and governance (2.15) present the highest-impact opportunities for transformation"
**Anti-pattern:** "Fraud & Risk leads at 2.82; Products at 2.11 is the highest-impact opportunity" ← semicolon format, drops second weakness, grammatically awkward

### Slide 16 — Opportunities (max 124 chars at 21pt — REQUIRES font reduction from 26pt)
**Pattern:** `[N spelled out] capability areas are ready for transformation — investing in them unlocks [outcome 1], [outcome 2], and [outcome 3]`
**Alliant:** "Three capability areas are ready for transformation — investing in them unlocks unified client service, enterprise data intelligence, and governed AI-powered innovation"
**Anti-pattern:** "3 capabilities ready for transformation — investing unlocks deposit growth and efficiency" ← uses numeral, drops "areas", drops "in them", only 2 outcomes

### Slide 20 — Close (max ~100 chars at ~19pt)
**Pattern:** `Share your feedback so we can deliver a refined maturity model and targeted capability map for [Client]'s [next session / leadership team]`
**Alliant:** "Share your feedback so we can deliver a refined maturity model and targeted capability map for Alliant's next session"
