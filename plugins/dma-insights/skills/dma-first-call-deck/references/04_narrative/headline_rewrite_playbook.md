# Zennify Headline Rewrite Playbook
## McKinsey-Style Systematic Methodology for Presentations

---

## I. The 4-Pattern System

### Pattern 1: Paradox
**Structure:** "[Actor] excels at [X], yet [unexpected Y] is the constraint"

**When to use:** When strengths and weaknesses coexist on the same slide; to create tension that demands resolution.

**Why it works:** Paradox headlines acknowledge complexity and prevent false simplicity. They signal sophistication to the audience.

**Examples:**

1. **Paradox - Financial Health**
   - "Pacific Coast CU leads peers in Customer Experience at 3.2, yet Data & Technology at 1.9 threatens to erode that advantage"

2. **Paradox - Growth Dynamics**
   - "Revenue grew 18% YoY, but rising CAC makes each new customer 40% more expensive to acquire"

3. **Paradox - Digital Capability**
   - "87% of members prefer digital channels, yet only 3 of 12 products are available online"

---

### Pattern 2: Quantified Impact
**Structure:** "[Situation] = [specific monetary/efficiency impact]"

**When to use:** When you can attach a dollar figure, time saving, or efficiency metric; creates concrete stakes.

**Why it works:** Quantified impact removes ambiguity. A $3M problem feels different than a "significant" problem.

**Examples:**

1. **Quantified Impact - Member Lifecycle**
   - "12-day onboarding drives 28% abandonment — an estimated $3.2M in lost member lifetime value annually"

2. **Quantified Impact - Operational Inefficiency**
   - "Manual underwriting adds 4.5 days to loan processing, costing $890K/year in operational overhead"

3. **Quantified Impact - Capability Maturity**
   - "Three capability gaps account for 70% of the maturity deficit, representing $2.1M in unrealized efficiency"

---

### Pattern 3: Capability Gap → Outcome
**Structure:** "[N] capabilities close [X]% of the gap, unlocking [outcome]"

**When to use:** For opportunity/recommendation slides; to connect solution directly to measurable improvement.

**Why it works:** Shifts focus from problems to actionable solutions. Creates a pathway from diagnosis to results.

**Examples:**

1. **Gap Closure - Technology Modernization**
   - "Digital Onboarding, Analytics, and API Integration close 70% of the maturity gap within 16 weeks"

2. **Gap Closure - Operational Consolidation**
   - "Consolidating 4 CRM systems into FSC eliminates 12 hours/week of duplicate data entry per advisor"

3. **Gap Closure - Compliance & Risk**
   - "Automating 3 compliance workflows reduces regulatory risk exposure by an estimated 45%"

---

### Pattern 4: The Window
**Structure:** "[Actor] is positioned to [achieve X] by [date] if [constraint] is addressed"

**When to use:** For urgency/close/next-steps slides; to create forward momentum and deadline clarity.

**Why it works:** Combines aspiration with conditionality. Not a threat; a genuine opportunity with an expiration date.

**Examples:**

1. **Window - Benchmark Recovery**
   - "Pacific Coast CU can reach peer median (3.1) by Q4 2026 if the diagnostic is approved by March 15"

2. **Window - Competitive Pressure**
   - "Delaying past Q2 widens the peer gap as 60% of competitors have active digital transformation programs"

3. **Window - Timeline Dependency**
   - "Signing by Friday enables the team to begin discovery March 18, targeting go-live before fiscal year-end"

## II. The 5-Step Rewrite Process

### Step 1: Identify the Core Insight
**Task:** Extract the CLAIM, not the topic.

**The distinction:**
- **Topic** = what the slide is about (e.g., "Assessment Results", "Digital Capabilities")
- **Claim** = what you're arguing about the topic (e.g., "digital capability lags peer median by 40%")

**How to find it:**
- Read the slide content. What single assertion could a reasonable person debate?
- Avoid tautologies ("We measured maturity") and focus on findings ("We trail on speed").
- Ask: "If I remove everything except one sentence, what would it be?"

---

### Step 2: Choose the Pattern
**Decision tree:**

1. **Does the claim show strength + weakness coexisting?** → Use **Paradox**
2. **Can you attach a dollar figure, time, or efficiency %?** → Use **Quantified Impact**
3. **Are you proposing a solution that closes a gap?** → Use **Capability Gap → Outcome**
4. **Is the primary message about timing or urgency?** → Use **The Window**

**If multiple patterns fit:** Choose the one that best emphasizes the key stake for your audience (usually financial impact > capability impact > timeline pressure).

---

### Step 3: Draft Using the Pattern Formula
**Task:** Fill the template with your specific data.

**Process:**
- Do not paraphrase or soften the language.
- Use precise numbers, not ranges ("4.5 days" not "about a week").
- Name the actor explicitly (institution, team, system — not "the organization").
- Include a comparative or benchmark reference if available.

---

### Step 4: Specificity Test
**Does your headline contain all three elements?**
1. **A number** (score, dollar amount, percentage, time period)
2. **A client/entity name or system reference** (Pacific Coast CU, FSC, compliance workflows)
3. **A verb** (leads, trails, drives, closes, enables, risks)

**If missing any → Add or revise.**

**Weak:** "Digital capabilities need improvement"
- Missing number, missing comparative verb, missing entity clarity

**Strong:** "Pacific Coast CU trails peer median on Digital Capability (1.9 vs. 3.1), creating a 40% efficiency gap"
- Number ✓ (1.9, 3.1, 40%) | Entity ✓ (Pacific Coast CU) | Verb ✓ (trails, creating)

---

### Step 5: Arguability Test
**Could a reasonable person disagree with this headline?**

**If NO** → It's a truism, not an insight. Strengthen it.

**If YES** → It's defensible. Move forward.

**Examples of truisms to avoid:**
- "We assessed capabilities" (not arguable; it's a fact of the engagement)
- "Digital adoption is important" (not arguable; universally assumed)
- "The team will meet every week" (not arguable; it's a plan)

**How to strengthen:**
- Replace vague adjectives with numbers.
- Add a comparative ("vs. peer", "vs. baseline", "vs. industry").
- Specify the consequence or opportunity.

## III. Three Full Worked Examples

### Example 1: DMA Assessment Results Slide
**Starting point:** Slide labeled "Assessment Results"
**Content:** A 4x4 matrix showing Pacific Coast CU's maturity scores (1.9–3.2 range) versus peer median (2.8–3.4 range).

#### Step 1: Identify Core Insight
Reading the data:
- CU scores highest on Customer Experience (3.2, above peer 3.0)
- CU scores lowest on Data & Technology (1.9, below peer 3.1)
- This is a paradox: strength in CX, weakness in enabling capability

**Core claim:** "CU's customer experience excellence is structurally unsustainable because the technology infrastructure can't scale it."

#### Step 2: Choose Pattern
Multiple patterns fit, but **Paradox** best captures the tension and urgency. A "Window" headline would work, but Paradox is more diagnostic at this stage.

#### Step 3: Draft Using Pattern
Template: "[Actor] excels at [X], yet [unexpected Y] is the constraint"

**Draft:** "Pacific Coast CU leads peers in Customer Experience (3.2), yet Data & Technology capability at 1.9 threatens to erode that advantage"

#### Step 4: Specificity Test
- Number ✓ (3.2, 1.9, implied peer median)
- Entity ✓ (Pacific Coast CU)
- Verb ✓ (leads, threatens)

All present. Consider whether adding the peer benchmark makes it stronger:

**Revised:** "Pacific Coast CU leads peers in Customer Experience (3.2 vs. 3.0 median), yet Data & Technology at 1.9 (vs. 3.1 median) threatens to erode that advantage"

#### Step 5: Arguability Test
**Can someone disagree?** Yes. They might argue that CX and tech are independent, or that the tech gap is manageable. That's good — it's a defensible but debatable claim.

**FINAL HEADLINE:**
"Pacific Coast CU leads peers in Customer Experience (3.2 vs. 3.0 median), yet Data & Technology at 1.9 (vs. 3.1 median) threatens to erode that advantage"

---

### Example 2: Sales Discovery Readout Slide
**Starting point:** Slide labeled "Discovery Readout Overview"
**Content:** Narrative and bullet points describing pain points: 87% member preference for digital, 12-day onboarding, 28% digital abandonment rate, estimated $3.2M annual member value leakage.

#### Step 1: Identify Core Insight
The discovery unearthed a specific quantifiable problem: not just that digital is slow, but that the slowness translates to lost lifetime value at scale.

**Core claim:** "The long onboarding process is the primary driver of member lifecycle leakage, and it's costing millions annually."

#### Step 2: Choose Pattern
**Quantified Impact** is the perfect fit. This is a situation-to-cost headline.

#### Step 3: Draft Using Pattern
Template: "[Situation] = [specific monetary/efficiency impact]"

**Draft:** "12-day member onboarding drives 28% abandonment, equal to an estimated $3.2M in lost lifetime value annually"

#### Step 4: Specificity Test
- Number ✓ (12 days, 28%, $3.2M)
- Entity ✓ (implied: member onboarding at Pacific Coast CU; could be more explicit)
- Verb ✓ (drives, equal)

Consider adding the CU name for absolute clarity:

**Revised:** "Pacific Coast CU's 12-day onboarding drives 28% member abandonment, costing an estimated $3.2M in annual lifetime value"

#### Step 5: Arguability Test
**Can someone disagree?** Yes. They might argue that abandonment has multiple causes, or that the LTV estimate is conservative. That's defensible disagreement. The headline is strong.

**FINAL HEADLINE:**
"Pacific Coast CU's 12-day onboarding drives 28% member abandonment, costing an estimated $3.2M in annual lifetime value"

---

### Example 3: Roadmap Slide
**Starting point:** Slide labeled "Roadmap"
**Content:** A phased timeline showing initiatives (Digital Onboarding in Phase 1, Analytics Platform in Phase 2, API Integration in Phase 3), with delivery targets spanning 16 weeks and a note that the CU wants to close maturity gaps by fiscal year-end (FYE).

#### Step 1: Identify Core Insight
The roadmap is not just a sequence of activities; it's a vehicle for closing the capability gap and hitting a business deadline.

**Core claim:** "If we execute these three initiatives on schedule, we unlock the ability to reach peer-equivalent maturity before fiscal year-end, but only if we start immediately."

#### Step 2: Choose Pattern
**The Window** is the best fit. There is a deadline, a condition (approval/kickoff), and a contingent outcome.

#### Step 3: Draft Using Pattern
Template: "[Actor] is positioned to [achieve X] by [date] if [constraint] is addressed"

**Draft:** "Pacific Coast CU can close 70% of the maturity gap and reach peer median by FYE if roadmap execution begins by March 18"

#### Step 4: Specificity Test
- Number ✓ (70%, FYE date, March 18 kickoff)
- Entity ✓ (Pacific Coast CU)
- Verb ✓ (can close, reach)

Strong. Consider whether "reach peer median" or "reach 3.1 on Data & Technology" is more precise. The former is more narrative; the latter is more diagnostic. Stay with the narrative version for a forward-looking roadmap slide.

#### Step 5: Arguability Test
**Can someone disagree?** Yes. They might argue that 16 weeks is insufficient, or that FYE is too aggressive. That's the point — this headline creates debate that the roadmap then resolves. It's exactly what you want at this stage.

**FINAL HEADLINE:**
"Pacific Coast CU can close 70% of the maturity gap and reach peer median by FYE if roadmap execution begins by March 18"

## IV. Framework → Headline Tone Mapping

Use this table to align headline style with the communication framework you're using.

| **Framework** | **Tone** | **Typical Pattern** | **Verb Style** | **When to Use** | **Example** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Consultant (SCQA)** | Logical, data-first, comparative | "[Actor] [status] [relative to benchmark]" | Shows, trails, leads, scores, exceeds, ranks | Diagnostic presentations; assessment readouts; when peer comparison is key | "Acme scores 2.4/5 on Digital Capability, trailing peers by 0.6 across all four pillars" |
| **Storyteller (Sparkline)** | Emotional, tension-release, aspirational | "[Current state] → [Future state]" | Transforms, enables, unlocks, envisions, breaks free, transcends | Transformational initiatives; vision slideshows; when narrative arc matters more than numbers | "12-day onboarding today; 10-minute digital onboarding tomorrow, unlocking $3.2M in captured member value" |
| **Sales (AIDA)** | Urgent, impact-driven, capability-forward | "[Action] [because] [impact]" | Closes, captures, accelerates, protects, secures, unlocks | Closing presentations; when timeline pressure exists; when decision-readiness is the goal | "Closing the digital gap by Q2 protects $12M in member value at risk and positions CU ahead of 70% of competitors" |

**How to choose:**
- **SCQA (Consultant):** Use in diagnostic decks, assessment reads, and detailed findings decks. Audience expects rigor and peer benchmarking.
- **Sparkline (Storyteller):** Use in vision decks, transformation overviews, and internal alignment meetings. Audience needs emotional buy-in.
- **AIDA (Sales):** Use in closing decks, final recommendations, and executive steering committee meetings. Audience is deciding whether to proceed.

## V. Source-Anchored Reframing Rules

### ALLOWED Reframing
These transforms preserve semantic meaning while improving clarity:

1. **Directional claims from data**
   - Source: "CU's score is 1.9; peer median is 3.1"
   - Reframed: "CU trails peers by 1.2 points on Data & Technology"
   - Status: ✓ Allowed (data-driven directional claim)

2. **Calculated deltas**
   - Source: "CU scores 1.9, peers average 3.1; efficiency literature suggests each 1-point gap = ~15% operational drag"
   - Reframed: "0.6-point gap implies ~10% efficiency deficit"
   - Status: ✓ Allowed (transparent mathematical inference)

3. **Inferred urgency from trends**
   - Source: "Competitor analysis shows 60% of peers launched digital platforms in past 12 months"
   - Reframed: "Competitive digital adoption is accelerating, creating time pressure"
   - Status: ✓ Allowed (trend-based urgency, not invented)

4. **Benchmark comparisons**
   - Source: "Industry data shows median DMA maturity is 3.0; CU is 1.9"
   - Reframed: "Below the 3.0 industry median"
   - Status: ✓ Allowed (direct benchmark reference)

---

### PROHIBITED Reframing
These transforms add meaning NOT supported by source data:

1. **Emotional adjectives absent from source**
   - Prohibited: "Dangerously low Data & Technology capability (1.9)"
   - Why: "Dangerously" is editorial judgment not in source
   - What to do instead: Quantify the risk ("…threatens $3.2M in captured member value")

2. **Invented consequences**
   - Prohibited: "Data & Technology gaps are causing rapid market share loss"
   - Why: Source shows a capability gap, not market share loss
   - What to do instead: Link to specific, quantified consequences from your source ("…drives 28% digital abandonment, equal to $3.2M annual LTV leakage")

3. **Added moral judgment**
   - Prohibited: "CU must act now on digital transformation"
   - Why: "Must" is prescriptive; source is diagnostic
   - What to do instead: Frame as conditional opportunity ("CU can reach peer median by FYE if execution begins by March 18")

4. **Extrapolated projections not in source data**
   - Prohibited: "CU will fall further behind unless data infrastructure is upgraded within 90 days"
   - Why: Source doesn't forecast a specific failure date
   - What to do instead: Reference actual competitive trends or timeline constraints from your engagement scope ("60% of peers have launched platforms; CU's delayed start narrows the window to Q2 2026")

---

### THE TEST: Source Defensibility
**Before finalizing any headline, ask:**
"Could I point to a specific sentence or chart in my source material that directly supports this claim?"

**If YES** → You're source-anchored. Proceed.

**If NO** → You've invented meaning. Either find the source data to support it, or soften/remove the claim.

**Example of the test in action:**

| **Headline Candidate** | **Source Present?** | **Verdict** | **Fix** |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Data gap is critically limiting growth" | No source quantifies growth impact | ✗ Prohibited | Change to: "Data & Technology gap (1.9 vs. 3.1 peer median) correlates with 28% digital abandonment, costing $3.2M annually" |
| "CU will lose market share if it doesn't modernize by Q3" | No source forecasts Q3 failure | ✗ Prohibited | Change to: "Delaying digital modernization past Q2 risks further competitive divergence; 60% of peers have active programs" |
| "Digital Onboarding, Analytics, and API Integration close 70% of maturity gap" | Yes: gap analysis + roadmap phasing | ✓ Allowed | Proceed to publication |

## VI. Headline Fatigue Warning & Countermeasures

### The Problem
In presentations longer than 7 slides, headlines tend to deteriorate into **labels** by slide 8 onward. Examples:

- Slide 1: "Pacific Coast CU trails peers on Data & Technology, threatening its CX advantage" (strong)
- Slide 2: "Digital Onboarding capability close 70% of the gap by Q2" (strong)
- Slide 3: "Roadmap execution begins March 18" (label, not insight)
- Slide 4: "Budget and resource requirements" (label)
- Slide 5: "Next steps and timeline" (label)

**Why it happens:** Fatigue, time pressure, and the mistaken belief that later slides need only informational headers.

**Why it matters:** A weak headline on slide 8 breaks the narrative momentum you've built over 7 strong slides. The audience stops tracking your argument.

---

### Countermeasures

#### Countermeasure 1: Draft All Headlines Upfront
**Process:**
1. Before building any slides, write out the headline for EVERY slide in your deck.
2. Use the 5-step process for each one.
3. Review the full headline sequence as a narrative arc (Does it tell a coherent story?).
4. Only then begin designing slides.

**Why it works:** You catch headline gaps and fatigue patterns before you've invested design time.

---

#### Countermeasure 2: Review the Last 3 Headlines Specifically
**Process:**
1. After drafting all headlines, isolate the final 3.
2. Ask: "Are these still carrying the argument, or have they become labels?"
3. If they're labels, reapply the Capability Gap → Outcome pattern (it's the easiest to apply to detail slides and keeps the narrative alive).

**Example repair:**

| **Original (label)** | **Repaired (insight)** |
|---|---|
| "Implementation timeline" | "Staggered rollout of Digital Onboarding in Q1 and Analytics in Q2 minimizes operational disruption while delivering core capability improvements within 16 weeks" |
| "Budget requirements" | "Targeted investment of $1.8M in Year 1 closes 70% of maturity gap, generating $3.2M in captured member lifetime value by FYE" |

---

#### Countermeasure 3: Apply the Storyline Test to Late Slides
**Process:**
1. After drafting your full deck, read ONLY the headlines in sequence (skip the slide body).
2. Ask: "Do these headlines alone tell a coherent story? Could a busy executive understand the argument from headlines only?"
3. If the answer is no for any slide after slide 7, that's a signal to strengthen the headline.

**Example of a failing headline sequence (slides 8–10):**
1. "Implementation timeline"
2. "Risk mitigation"
3. "Success metrics"

**Failing Storyline Test:** These are process headers, not narrative claims. They don't advance the argument.

**Repaired sequence:**
1. "Phased approach begins Q1 2026, targeting full capability deployment by Q3 and measurable member experience improvement by FYE"
2. "Three risk vectors mitigated through contingency roadmap and external resource augmentation, reducing delivery slippage risk below 5%"
3. "Early wins in digital adoption and processing speed will be visible in Q2; full ROI expected in Year 2"

**Passing Storyline Test:** Each headline advances the narrative (timeline → risk mitigation → outcome expectations).

---

#### Countermeasure 4: Assign Pattern Diversity
**Goal:** Avoid repetitive patterns in sequence.

**Pattern usage distribution for a 12-slide deck:**
- **Slides 1–4:** Paradox (diagnostic phase) = 2–3 slides
- **Slides 5–8:** Quantified Impact + Capability Gap → Outcome (solution phase) = 3–4 slides
- **Slides 9–12:** Window + Capability Gap → Outcome (close phase) = 3–4 slides

**Why it works:** Pattern variety keeps the headline language fresh and signals narrative progression to the audience.

## VII. Quick Reference: Pattern Decision Tree

**Use this when you're on the clock and need to pick a pattern in 30 seconds.**

```
START: You have a claim to headline.

Q1: Does your claim show a STRENGTH + WEAKNESS coexisting?
    YES → Use PARADOX
    NO → Go to Q2

Q2: Can you attach a DOLLAR FIGURE or EFFICIENCY METRIC?
    YES → Use QUANTIFIED IMPACT
    NO → Go to Q3

Q3: Are you proposing a SOLUTION that closes a GAP?
    YES → Use CAPABILITY GAP → OUTCOME
    NO → Go to Q4

Q4: Is the primary message about TIMING or URGENCY?
    YES → Use THE WINDOW
    NO → Re-examine your claim. It may not be an insight yet.
```

## VIII. Headline Quality Checklist

Use this before finalizing any headline:

**Specificity (3 points)**
- [ ] Contains at least one number (score, %, $, days, date)
- [ ] Names the actor or entity explicitly
- [ ] Uses a strong verb (leads, trails, drives, closes, enables, threatens)

**Arguability (2 points)**
- [ ] A reasonable person could disagree
- [ ] It's not a tautology (e.g., "We assessed capabilities")

**Source Defensibility (2 points)**
- [ ] You can point to a sentence or chart in source material that supports it
- [ ] You haven't added emotional adjectives, invented consequences, or extrapolated unsupported projections

**Narrative Fit (2 points)**
- [ ] It advances the story (not just informational)
- [ ] It connects to the headline that came before and sets up the one that follows

**Clarity (1 point)**
- [ ] Reads naturally (not awkward or overstuffed with modifiers)

**Total: 9 points. Score: 7+ = Ready to publish.**

## IX. Common Pitfalls & How to Avoid Them

### Pitfall 1: The Vague Comparative
**Weak:** "CU needs to improve its digital capabilities"
**Why it fails:** No number, no benchmark, no verb with direction

**Strong:** "CU scores 1.9 on Digital Capability, 1.2 points below peer median, driving 28% member digital abandonment"

---

### Pitfall 2: The Truism
**Weak:** "Digital transformation is critical for success"
**Why it fails:** No one would argue this; it's not a claim

**Strong:** "Competitors are 12 months ahead on digital deployment; delaying past Q2 widens the gap by an estimated 18–24 months of capability development"

---

### Pitfall 3: The Label Masquerading as a Headline
**Weak:** "Assessment methodology and timeline"
**Why it fails:** Informational only, not argumentative

**Strong:** "Four-week diagnostic engagement surfaces capability roadmap and three-phase execution plan, enabling Q1 2026 kickoff"

---

### Pitfall 4: The Overloaded Headline
**Weak:** "Pacific Coast CU's Data & Technology capability at 1.9, trailing peers by 1.2 points, threatens to erode its 3.2 customer experience score, which is above median, creating a paradox that requires immediate investment in digital infrastructure, analytics, and API integration to close the gap before fiscal year-end when competitive pressure from 60% of peers with active programs will have widened further"

**Why it fails:** Too many ideas. Reader loses the through-line.

**Strong (broken into 2 headlines):**
1. "CU leads peers in Customer Experience (3.2), yet Data & Technology at 1.9 threatens to erode that advantage"
2. "Closing the gap requires three core capabilities: Digital Onboarding, Analytics, and API Integration—all deployable by Q3 if execution begins March 18"

---

### Pitfall 5: The Invented Urgency
**Weak:** "CU must transform immediately to survive"
**Why it fails:** "Must" and "survive" are not in the source data

**Strong:** "60% of competitors have active digital initiatives; delaying past Q2 risks losing early-mover advantage and extends time-to-parity from 12 to 18+ months"

## X. Workflow: From Initial Insight to Final Headline

### Phase 1: Insight Extraction (5 minutes)
1. Read the slide's supporting research, data, or interview notes.
2. Identify the ONE claim that a reasonable person could debate.
3. Write it as a simple sentence: "[Actor] [condition/finding] [because/implication]"

### Phase 2: Pattern Selection (2 minutes)
1. Ask the decision tree questions.
2. Choose the best-fit pattern.

### Phase 3: Draft (3 minutes)
1. Fill the pattern template with your specific data.
2. Don't soften or hedge language.

### Phase 4: Specificity Check (2 minutes)
1. Verify number, entity, and verb are present.
2. Add if missing.

### Phase 5: Arguability & Source Defense (3 minutes)
1. Is it debatable? Is it source-anchored?
2. Revise if not.

### Total elapsed time: ~15 minutes per headline

**Pro tip:** Batch headlines. Draft 3–4 headlines in succession, then review them as a group. Pattern recognition is easier in batches.

## XII. Tone Calibration by Audience

Adjust headline emphasis based on who's reading:

| **Audience** | **Emphasis** | **Example Adjustment** |
|---|---|---|
| **CFO/Board** | Quantified impact and financial outcome | "70% capability gap closure generates $3.2M in captured member value within 16 weeks" |
| **CTO/Operations** | Capability specificity and timeline | "Three capabilities—Digital Onboarding, Analytics, API Integration—deploy sequentially; full capability deployment by Q3 2026" |
| **CMO/Member Experience** | Member outcome and differentiation | "Digital Onboarding transforms member experience from 12-day friction to 10-minute frictionless enrollment, reducing abandonment from 28% to projected 8%" |
| **Executive Sponsor** | Window/urgency and next decision | "Competitor adoption accelerating; signing by Friday enables March 18 kickoff, closing the gap before 70% of market competitors do" |

## XIII. Final Checklist Before Publishing

- [ ] Headline passes Specificity Test (number + entity + verb)
- [ ] Headline passes Arguability Test (defensibly debatable)
- [ ] Headline passes Source Defensibility Test (can point to supporting data)
- [ ] Headline is in an approved pattern (one of the four)
- [ ] Headline is not a label (it advances narrative)
- [ ] Headline is concise (no more than two lines on slide)
- [ ] Headline matches audience tone (board, operations, member-facing, sponsor)
- [ ] Headline sequence maintains narrative arc (read all headlines; do they tell the story?)
- [ ] Late slides (8+) use Capability Gap → Outcome pattern to maintain engagement
- [ ] No emotional adjectives unsupported by source ("dangerously", "critically")
- [ ] No invented consequences or extrapolated projections

**If all boxes checked:** Headline is production-ready.
