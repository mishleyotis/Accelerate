## Credit Unions

### Regulatory Context
- NCUA examination cycle (12-18 months)
- CAMEL ratings impact on operational flexibility
- Field of membership expansion constraints
- Net worth ratio requirements
- CFPB consumer lending scrutiny (overdraft, fair lending)

### Competitive Dynamics
- Digital banks (Chime, SoFi) targeting similar demographics
- Fintech partnerships vs. build decisions
- Core processor oligopoly (Fiserv, Jack Henry, FIS)
- Shared branching and CO-OP ATM network effects
- Merger/consolidation pressure on smaller CUs

### Capability Nuances
- P1C5 ESG: Member value focus vs. shareholder return (cooperative model)
- P2C2 Onboarding: SEG-based membership verification adds complexity
- P3C3 Compliance: Consumer lending focus, overdraft scrutiny, fair lending
- P4C3 Architecture: Core modernization lag vs. digital-first entrants

### Language: Use "members" not "customers"; "credit union" not "bank"

## Regional Banks

### Regulatory Context
- Multiple regulators based on charter (OCC vs. FDIC vs. Fed)
- CRA examination and rating implications
- Stress testing requirements (if >$100B)
- BSA/AML heightened scrutiny
- Tailoring rules based on asset category

### Competitive Dynamics
- National banks' digital capabilities creating pressure
- Community bank consolidation opportunities
- Commercial real estate concentration risks
- Treasury management as differentiation opportunity
- Fintech competition in payments and lending

### Capability Nuances
- P1C2 Governance: Board sophistication varies widely by size
- P2C1 Marketing: Branch-centric vs. digital acquisition tension
- P3C2 Fraud: Check fraud and ACH fraud prevalence
- P4C1 Data: Multiple acquired systems create fragmented data landscape

### Language: Use "customers"; acknowledge dual consumer/commercial focus

## Commercial Lending

### Regulatory Context
- Leveraged lending guidance
- CECL implementation maturity
- Concentration risk management (CRE, industry)
- Syndicated loan documentation standards
- Interagency guidance on credit risk management

### Competitive Dynamics
- Private credit fund competition intensifying
- Direct lending platforms disrupting origination
- Relationship vs. transaction lending models
- Industry specialization as differentiation
- Technology-enabled credit decisioning

### Capability Nuances
- P1C1 Strategy: Industry vertical focus decisions critical
- P2C2 Onboarding: KYC/KYB complexity for business clients
- P3C1 Automation: Credit decisioning automation maturity is key differentiator
- P4C2 Analytics: Portfolio monitoring and early warning systems

### Language: Use "clients" not "customers"; B2B focus throughout

## Corporate & Investment Banks (CIB)

### Regulatory Context
- G-SIB surcharges and TLAC requirements
- Volcker Rule compliance
- Derivatives clearing and margin requirements (UMR)
- Cross-border regulatory complexity
- Resolution planning requirements

### Competitive Dynamics
- Bulge bracket consolidation
- Fintech disruption in payments and FX
- Electronic trading platform competition
- Prime brokerage differentiation
- DeFi and digital asset emergence

### Capability Nuances
- P1C2 Governance: Three lines of defense rigor expected at high level
- P2C4 Personalization: Institutional client customization (not retail personalization)
- P3C2 Fraud: Market surveillance and insider trading focus
- P4C3 Architecture: Legacy trading systems modernization is defining challenge

### Language: Use "clients"; distinguish institutional vs. corporate segments

## Insurance Carriers

### Regulatory Context
- State-by-state DOI examination (50+ jurisdictions)
- NAIC model law adoption variance across states
- RBC ratio requirements
- ORSA (Own Risk and Solvency Assessment)
- Rate filing and approval processes

### Competitive Dynamics
- Insurtech disruption (Lemonade, Root, Hippo) in personal lines
- Direct-to-consumer vs. agency distribution tension
- Embedded insurance partnerships emerging
- Parametric and usage-based product innovation
- Climate risk repricing pressure

### Capability Nuances
- P1C3 Innovation: Product development speed vs. actuarial rigor tension
- P2C2 Onboarding: Underwriting automation vs. selection quality tradeoff
- P3C1 Automation: Claims straight-through processing is primary efficiency lever
- P4C2 Analytics: Predictive modeling for pricing and claims is core competency

### Language: Use "policyholders" or "insureds"; distinguish P&C vs. Life/Health

## Insurance Brokerages

### Regulatory Context
- Producer licensing requirements (state-by-state)
- E&O coverage adequacy
- Fee disclosure requirements
- Fiduciary considerations (in some states)
- Transparency Act implications

### Competitive Dynamics
- Consolidation driven by Marsh, Aon, Willis dominance
- Digital quoting platforms disrupting commercial lines
- MGA/MGU opportunities for specialty risks
- Employee benefits complexity increasing
- Private equity-backed roll-up competition

### Capability Nuances
- P1C1 Strategy: Organic vs. M&A growth decisions dominate strategic planning
- P2C3 Omnichannel: Producer portal vs. client self-service balance
- P3C4 Resilience: Carrier relationship concentration risk unique to brokers
- P4C1 Data: Client data spread across multiple carrier systems

### Language: Use "clients"; distinguish commercial vs. personal lines, benefits vs. P&C

## Asset Management

### Regulatory Context
- Investment Company Act compliance
- ADV Part 2 disclosure requirements
- Custody rule requirements
- Derivatives rule implementation (Rule 18f-4)
- ESG disclosure requirements evolving

### Competitive Dynamics
- Fee compression (passive vs. active debate continues)
- Direct indexing disrupting traditional products
- ESG mandate proliferation and greenwashing scrutiny
- Institutional vs. retail distribution mix shift
- Alternative investment democratization

### Capability Nuances
- P1C5 ESG: Integration into investment process is table stakes, not differentiator
- P2C4 Personalization: Tax-loss harvesting automation, custom portfolio construction
- P3C3 Compliance: Trade surveillance and best execution obligations
- P4C2 Analytics: Factor modeling and attribution are core analytical capabilities

### Language: Use "investors" or "clients"; distinguish institutional vs. retail; use AUM not assets

## Wealth Managers / RIAs

### Regulatory Context
- Regulation Best Interest (Reg BI) for BDs
- Form CRS requirements
- SEC examination priorities (annual update matters)
- State fiduciary rules variance
- Custody rule amendments

### Competitive Dynamics
- Custodian consolidation (Schwab-TD) reducing choices
- RIA aggregator growth (Focus Financial, CI, Hightower)
- Robo-advisor competition on fees
- Succession planning pressures (aging advisor demographics)
- Hybrid RIA/BD models increasing

### Capability Nuances
- P1C2 Governance: Compliance infrastructure often undersized vs. firm complexity
- P2C2 Onboarding: Account transfer (ACAT) efficiency is client experience differentiator
- P3C3 Compliance: Suitability documentation and Reg BI compliance burden
- P4C3 Architecture: Custodian integration dependencies limit flexibility

### Language: Use "clients" and "advisors"; distinguish RIA vs. BD regulatory framework
