§0 · The owner's target, restated

Two data planes, and the agents write to the wrong one

After this audit was first assembled, the owner restated the target architecture in one diagram: a request arrives (Slack, or a typed Routine) → a Routine fires headless → the assessment runs end to end → everything publishes to the DMAI app — the six surfaces, the governance issue register, and exactly three output artifacts: the DMA Scoring Workbook (contract v3) that every agent records to, and the Client Research Report and Assessment Report curated from it after evidence synthesis.

This audit had measured the system against its own internal contracts. Re-measured against the owner's target, the deepest finding changes shape: it is not that individual mechanisms are defective — it is that the pipeline runs on a parallel JSON plane the owner never designated, and the workbook the owner designates is a terminal export nothing reasons over.

The front-of-pipeline absences (no front door, no evidence-mode field, no internal-document ingest) stand unchanged. And the publication-gap owner decision on invariant 2 becomes sharper, not different: the three artifacts the owner requires published to the app are exactly the files the connector's 33 JSON-only tools cannot carry.

What the thirteen defects actually bought

PR #2's central admission is that every one of its defects was reported from a rendered page, by a person, after the connector had said PASS. Each produced a gate. I tested whether those gates catch the defect or the sentence, by feeding each one a paraphrase that is plainly the same defect.

The pattern is the finding: the defects were found by a person generalising, and the gates encode the string that was reported, not the judgement that found it. Attended, that is fine — the next paraphrase is caught by the same person and becomes the next gate. Unattended, the generalising step is gone.

Grant the search connectors to the Routines, or build the search_requests relay the preamble already promises. Why first: everything in Stages 5 and 6 is downstream of evidence. Shipping the v4.2 reasoning layer buys nothing while the questions cannot be asked. It is also the only item here that is primarily a configuration change rather than engineering.

2 · Make one gate check correctness rather than consistency

Start with the two one-line fixes whose correct constants already exist, with comments explaining why they exist: use READINESS_DEFAULT ("amber") instead of the literal "green" at validation2.py:1482, and count distinct origins rather than distinct domains at register.py:95. Why second: these break legs 1 and 4 of the eight-leg path that passes all 69 gates. The first alone drops the maximum fit a readiness-silent card can reach from 99.0 to 85.0. Neither needs a design decision.

3 · Make a run survive its own container

Persist $RUN, and fix ledger.py stats. Why third, not first: it is what makes the other two hold under real unattended operation rather than what unblocks them. A pipeline that can search and can refuse a wrong number still loses the work when the container exits, and still cannot say it is stuck — there is no terminal run state, no run-level clock, and the four watchdogs that exist are named in zero of three live Routine prompts and zero CI steps.

What is deliberately not on this list

Shipping v4.2 into the plugin. It is a small job and the right thing to do — but its own reasoning layer carries the defects measured in Stages 5 and 6: an all-STUB synthesis passes floors_gate --require-synthesis, proxy-only evidence closes as FACT and publishes as M4 with HIGH confidence, and G1–G12 each mean two different things. Shipping it before item 1 delivers rigour that cannot reach evidence; before item 2, it adds a second gate family with the same blind spot as the first.
