Quality assurance

# QA report

An adversarial pass over the five documents and the 37 synthesis prompts they depend on. This is not a review for typos — it looks for the failure modes this product is specifically designed against: contradictions between sources, one concept wearing two names, numbers asserted rather than computed, and prompts that cannot produce what the surface requires.

## Method

Check | What it looks for

Computed-count audit | Every countable claim in prose traced back to the counts module. A number typed by hand is a number that will drift.

Terminology drift | One concept with two names, measured on prose with code identifiers excluded so ccg_catalog_version does not masquerade as a spelling error.

Concept grounding | Every named mechanism defined somewhere, and not used exactly once and abandoned.

Source-spec coverage | Twenty load-bearing requirements from the two specifications, each traced to at least one document.

Cross-document contradiction | The same quantity asserted differently in two places.

Register integrity | Every surface in the register present in the source specification, and every spec surface in the register.

Prompt completeness | All 37 prompts scored against 14 attributes a synthesis agent needs to produce correctly first time and repair second time.

Producer-to-render handoff | Whether the prompt that owns a section actually specifies what the surface renders.

The headline
The documents hold up structurally — counts reconcile, coverage is complete, navigation works. What does not hold up is the prompt layer. Prompt quality ranges from 3 of 14 attributes to 14 of 14, audience marking appears in 5 prompts of 37, and three surfaces carry vocabularies that contradict either the prototype or each other. A synthesis agent working from the weaker prompts cannot produce what the surface requires.

Findings · blocking

# Ten findings that would produce wrong output

B-01 The stack layer vocabulary contradicts itself four ways Blocking

Four sources, four different layer sets.

Source | Layers

Prototype LAYER_LABEL | Operations & core banking, Customer engagement, Data & analytics, Infrastructure & cloud 4

Spec, what-must-be-presented | core, CRM, data, integration, channel 5

Spec, sources table and prompt | core, crm, data, integration, channel, security 6

The documents as first written | the prototype's four 4

Resolution. Adopt the prototype's four, because they carry pillar tags and a primary-gap marker the other vocabularies do not, and because they are what actually renders. The spec's five- and six-layer lists are superseded. The T1 prompt must be reissued against the four.

B-02 Stack layer keys collide with the evidence-level vocabulary Blocking

The prototype keys its four layers L2 L3 L4 L5. The evidence-level ladder governing what verb the prose may use is L1 L2 L3 L4. Both render on the same card. layer: "L4" means Data & analytics; evidence_level: "L4" means a single weak source.

The prototype's own source carries the comment “Natural layer labels (no L1-L5)” — the collision was noticed and worked around in the labels rather than in the keys.

Resolution. Re-cut the layer keys to OPS · CUST · DATA · INFRA. This is the same class of defect as three colliding G-numbered gate families, and it is fixed the same way: make the identifier unambiguous in isolation.

B-03 Two different things are both called safeguard gates Blocking

The MCP specification's SG family runs at submit and produces pass, fail or not-run with a plain-language label, rendered to the client. The Surface specification's H5 is the caps the assessment applied — cap-log rows, uncertainty bands, QA holds, analyst overrides — and its prompt emits {gate_id, kind, ceiling, affected_categories[], rationale, e_ids[]} with no result field and no plain label.

They share a section name, a surface and a tab. As prompted, H5 cannot render what the documents require of a safeguard gate, because the prompt never asks for a result.

Resolution. One section, two arrays. heatmap.safeguard_gates carries caps[] — what the assessment applied — and gates[] — the SG results with plain labels and an explicit not-run state. Both render on the Health tab under their own headings. The prompt must be reissued to emit both.

B-04 The producing prompt for the synthesis drawer does not specify the synthesis Blocking

H2 owns heatmap.cell_evidence, which is the section the synthesis drawer renders from. Its prompt is 642 characters and covers linking only. The actual content contract — grain check before anything else, evidence list first, a 40–90 word synthesis, the count printed beside it, the peer fallback ladder — lives in the DD-1 drilldown prompt.

An agent reading the H2 prompt produces a section with linkage and no synthesis. An agent reading DD-1 has no section to submit into, because drilldowns are not submission units.

Resolution. Fold DD-1's content contract into H2. A drilldown that renders from a section must not hold requirements the section's own prompt lacks.

B-05 The landscape strip cannot be computed from what the register emits Blocking

T2 renders four counts — confirmed, inferred, claimed and gaps — and the documents require them to be recomputed from the T1 register rather than stored. T1's prompt emits {vendor, product, layer, confidence, as_of, e_ids[]}: no status field. The prototype renders three statuses (confirmed, inferred, absent) and no claimed at all.

Resolution. T1 emits status from CONFIRMED · INFERRED · CLAIMED · ABSENT, with gaps on the landscape strip being the absent count. Without a status on every register row the strip is uncomputable, and a stored count would be a second source of truth for a number that already exists.

B-06 The peer fallback ladder is load-bearing and lives only inside two prompts Blocking

Where the peer table lacks a figure, the sanctioned procedure is a five-rung ladder ending in an explicit refusal. It determines peer_basis, peer_n and proxy_disclosure on every score row, and it is the mechanism that prevents peer fabrication — the failure the O1 prompt calls out by name.

It appeared in full in the O1 and DD-1 prompts and nowhere in the technical documents. A reader of the TRD would not know the ladder exists, and a second implementation would invent a different one.

Resolution. Specified formally in the TRD and reproduced in the Surface Specification. Every prompt that touches a peer figure references it rather than restating it.

B-07 M5 Transformational is unreachable Blocking

The prototype's maturityLabel and maturityHex have four branches. Anything at or above 4.0 returns Differentiating. The design set publishes a fifth band and a hex for it (#185F60) that no code path can reach.

A score of 4.6 renders as Differentiating. Prose that calls it Transformational contradicts the card beside it, and a payload that emits the word writes a band no renderer produces.

Resolution. The band domain is four values and is enforced as an enumerated type, so a fifth cannot be written. Prose uses the four reachable words. A genuine M5 in the workbook renders as Differentiating and is recorded as a parser observation rather than silently relabelled.

B-08 Two hex values for one band Blocking

M2 Building is #62D7B8 in the prototype resolver and #B0EDD3 in the published palette. Same band, two colours, and the palette is what a designer would build from.

Resolution. The resolver is authoritative because it is what renders. Nothing is served wrongly today — the payload carries no hex — but the two must be reconciled before anyone builds from the documentation. This is the same two-implementations-of-one-rule shape that rendered every directory score in brand teal.

B-09 Two freshness vocabularies collide on the word STALE Blocking

The evidence ladder bands at 12/24/36/48 months — CURRENT, RECENT, DATED, STALE, ARCHIVAL. The prototype's freshnessOf bands at 6/12 months — Current, Aging, Stale. An item at fourteen months is RECENT on the evidence ladder and Stale on the freshness dot, and both render on the same screen.

Resolution. The evidence ladder governs everything the payload emits — recency tags, tier weighting, the rank score, the age tracker. The dot is a UI affordance with a tighter threshold and must be relabelled so one word does not carry two meanings. Prose never uses the word: it states the age and lets the band speak.

B-10 The reasoning layer exists in exactly one prompt Blocking

The P1 platform prompt carries a full five-step reasoning protocol and calls it "STEP 7 — THE REASONING LAYER (R-Layer, and it is the point of this page)." State the rank-1 claim, argue the runner-up explicitly, test domain plausibility, run failure probes, then accept, reject or mark uncertain.

It appears in one prompt of thirty-seven. Every other surface making a ranked, causal or comparative claim — findings, insights, ceilings, recommendations, cohort patterns — has no equivalent. Those surfaces get gates, and every gate in this system checks a claim after it has been written.

Resolution. The R-Layer becomes a standing protocol with probe sets for ten surfaces, nine contradiction classes and a recorded verdict in the payload. It is the only mechanism that catches a claim which is well-formed, correctly cited, grain-locked and wrong.

## Deployment verification — Google Cloud

The target is Cloud Run. Every named tool was checked against current Google Cloud availability; four findings required changes and the rest verified clean.

ID | Finding | Disposition

D-01  |
Self-hosted PgBouncer named as the pooler
Cloud SQL ships Managed Connection Pooling — PgBouncer-based, transaction and session modes, ports 6432 and 3307 — which removes a container to patch. It requires the Enterprise Plus edition, a constraint the documents now state, with a sidecar fallback if the edition ever changes.  | Fixed TRD §16

D-02  |
IAM tokens expire under pooled connections
IAM database auth tokens live one hour; a pooled server connection that outlives its token fails intermittently with SQLSTATE 08P01 — a documented issue. Mitigated with automatic IAM auth through the Cloud SQL Python Connector, pool_recycle=1800 and pool_pre_ping.  | Fixed TRD §16, Plan

D-03  |
Compression was implied to be platform behaviour
Cloud Run does not compress dynamic responses and neither does the load balancer. The Brotli-or-gzip behaviour is FastAPI middleware, and the eight-to-one heatmap ratio depends on it actually being installed.  | Fixed TRD §16, §19

D-04  |
The HNSW build mechanism misstated
The index is created once at migration; the graph grows incrementally as each run's vectors insert. The cost claim was right — build effort lands at ingest, off the critical path — but the mechanism as stated would have someone dropping and recreating an index per run.  | Fixed TRD, Schema, Plan

D-05  |
Verified clean, no change
pgvector 0.6+ with HNSW ships on Cloud SQL, and SET LOCAL hnsw.ef_search is an ordinary GUC. PostgreSQL 16 is available. The 300-second promote fits Cloud Run's timeout with an order of magnitude to spare. The worker and migrator map to Cloud Run Jobs. Memorystore for Redis reaches Cloud Run over Direct VPC egress. The embedding model bundles in-image at ~90MB with no Vertex AI dependency.  | Checked

Claude Code and Claude Cowork are different things in this system
Claude Cowork is where synthesis runs: an agent reads the assessment through the MCP connector and writes the content the product serves. Claude Code is where the application is built: the implementation plan is written for it, and it never touches client content. A document that uses them interchangeably is describing two different trust boundaries as one.

## The model prompt

O6 Top findings scores 14 of 14 and is the template the rest should follow. Its structure:

```
STEP 1 — RETRIEVE   Read the reports in full. The analyst's findings already
                exist; extract them with their reasoning intact.
                Record source_kind=retrieved with section and page.

STEP 2 — DERIVE, ONLY IF RETRIEVAL YIELDS FEWER THAN 5
                Derive from the joins, not the scores.
                NEVER derive by taking the widest score gaps — that produces
                a sorted list, not findings.

STEP 3 — EMIT       Every field named in one block, each with its own budget
                and a measured exemplar.

STEP 4 — RANK       By strategic alignment, not gap width. And a stated
                fallback: if the objectives cannot be established, say so,
                rank by downstream impact, set ranking_basis=impact_fallback.

STEP 5 — COHESION   The five must read as one story. If they do not, you have
                five observations.

STEP 6 — DRILLDOWN  What the expanded panel carries, per heading.
```

Four things it does that the weak prompts do not: it prefers retrieval to derivation and says so; it gives every field a measured exemplar rather than an abstract instruction; it names a ranking key and a fallback; and it requires the set to cohere rather than merely to be complete.

Prompt engineering

# The prompt standard

Fourteen attributes. A prompt missing any of them is asking the agent to guess, and a guess that type-checks promotes silently wrong content.

# | Attribute | Requirement

1 | Numbered steps | A long prompt without steps is a wall the agent skims. Retrieval first, derivation only on failure.

2 | Output shape | Every field named, in one brace block. Intent without shape forces a guess that type-checks.

3 | Word budgets | Per field. Unbudgeted prose overflows its card or reads as filler.

4 | Named gates | What will be asserted at submit, before the agent writes.

5 | Empty state | What to emit when the evidence is not there, and what to record about the search.

6 | Explicit prohibitions | The commonest defects are things the agent should not have done.

7 | Identity gate | Every figure is about THIS entity. One contaminated figure reaches five surfaces.

8 | Grain lock | A quoted figure and its named cell are the same cell, within 0.05.

9 | Citation discipline | Register before citing; verbatim excerpts; ids the server allocated.

10 | Register rules | A client reads this prose. No consultant register, no codes, no score-predicate openers.

11 | Measured exemplar | A prompt anchored to a real defect is followed; an abstract one is not.

12 | Provenance | So deterministically composed content cannot present as analyst judgement.

13 | Audience marking | Which paths go in internal_only. An unmarked internal rung reaches the client.

14 | Ordering | Where order carries meaning — ranked, sequenced or chronological.

## Four standing clauses

Four attributes are identical on every surface and should be standing text the contract appends rather than something each prompt author remembers. That is what takes audience marking from five prompts of 37 to all of them.

```
STANDING · IDENTITY
Every figure, quote, document and source domain is about THIS entity.
Assert legal name, regulator, footprint, source domain and order of
magnitude. On failure: quarantine the field, emit the reason, escalate.
NEVER substitute a plausible value and NEVER render a partial identity.

STANDING · GRAIN
Any "<label> at N/5" you write must resolve to a served cell within 0.05.
Read the label and the score from the SAME row. Round once.

STANDING · REGISTER
A client reads this. No consultant register (leverage, journey,
best-in-class). No deficit framing (lacks, fails to). No raw taxonomy
codes in prose. No score-predicate openers. No markdown in a text field.
The verb is governed by the evidence level: L1 implemented · L2 selected ·
L3 likely uses · L4 may use.

STANDING · AUDIENCE
List in internal_only every JSON path the customer must not receive:
rank scores, tier weights, scoring rationale, synthetic provenance,
internal codes. Thin-evidence and quarantine markers are NOT internal —
honesty renders to the client.
```

## The required form

```
STEP 1 — RETRIEVE    Where the answer already exists, and how to record
                 that it was retrieved rather than derived.
STEP 2 — DERIVE      Only on retrieval failure. State what derivation is
                 legitimate and what looks like derivation but is not.
STEP 3 — EMIT        Every field in one brace block. Per-field budget.
                 A measured exemplar per prose field.
STEP 4 — ORDER       The ranking or sequencing key, and its fallback.
STEP 5 — ABSENCE     What to emit when the evidence is not there, and what
                 to record about the search that established it.
STEP 6 — GATES       What will be asserted at submit.
STEP 7 — DRILLDOWN   What the expanded panel carries, if the surface has one.
```

Why the measured exemplar matters more than the instruction
“Quantify the consequence” produces hedged prose. “Blocks 34 downstream subcaps”, “5–7 month cycle compression”, “Window closes at nCino go-live” produce the thing itself. The strongest prompt in the set gives an exemplar for every prose field, and it is the only one that scores full marks.

## The peer fallback ladder

Referenced by every prompt that touches a peer figure, restated by none of them.

# | Rung | Procedure | Records

1 | Peer table figure | peer_comparison_table.csv carries the cell | basis = table

2 | Recompute at lower cohort size | Drop the peer lacking the figure and re-take the median. Floor of three: N=5 → sorted[2]; N=4 → mean of sorted[1..2]; N=3 → sorted[1]. | basis = recomputed, peer_n records the size actually used

3 | Adjacency inference | Infer from a neighbouring sub-capability in the same category. | basis = inferred, labelled INFERENCE with one clause of reasoning

4 | Proxy ceiling | A signal that caps rather than estimates — e.g. a specialist-headcount ratio below 5% caps its category, negative-dominant review sentiment caps at mid-scale. Lowest cap wins. | basis = inferred, proxy_disclosure carries the literal phrase 'peer proxy'

5 | Stop | Print 'Cannot reliably estimate'. | basis = cannot_estimate, peer_median stays NULL

Never impute a value into the cell. The disclosure must carry the literal phrase peer proxy, because a governance check greps for it — and must never claim identical methodology. A proxied figure that reads as a measured one is the peer-fabrication failure this ladder exists to prevent.

## Standing recommendations

- Make the four standing clauses contract text, not prompt text. If the connector appends them to every section's doc, audience marking goes from five prompts of 37 to all of them without anyone remembering.
- Score every new prompt against the fourteen attributes before it ships. The check is mechanical and catches the gap class that produced six unusable prompts.
- Require a measured exemplar for every prose field. It is the single strongest differentiator between the prompts that work and the ones that do not.
- Treat a prompt under 1,500 characters for a non-trivial surface as a defect, not a style. Every prompt below that threshold failed at least seven attributes.
- Re-run this analysis in continuous integration. The matrix is computed from the prompt text, so it can regress silently.

The finding behind the findings
Eight of the ten blocking issues are the same shape: one name, two meanings — two layer vocabularies, layer keys colliding with evidence levels, safeguard gates meaning two different things, a section contract split across two prompts. This is precisely the defect class the specifications were written to eliminate, reappearing one level down in the prompt layer. The prompts need the same discipline the payload contracts already have: an identifier that is unambiguous in isolation, and a single place where each rule lives.
