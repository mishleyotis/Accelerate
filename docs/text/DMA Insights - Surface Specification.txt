# Overview — surface contracts

The page an AE is guaranteed to read. It answers one question — what is true about this client, and what do I lead with — and it is the only client page with a customer variant that renders rather than locks. Thirteen surfaces, twelve sections, and five of them are confidence instrumentation.

## O1 · Scores & peer benchmarks

Payload section overview.scores · component ScoreRing

Contract. Composite to 1dp with its band word, four pillar scores with peer deltas, posture chip, evidence basis, and an 18–32 word framing sentence that states the gap, quantifies it and localises it.

### What must be presented

- Overall maturity to 1dp in the hero ring, with its band word (Building, Competitive…).
- Four pillar scores P1–P4 as a bar strip, each to 1dp with peer delta arrow.
- Peer median per pillar and the cohort the median is drawn from.
- Every number must equal the served cell to within one 2dp rounding step. The hero ring and the run row must render the SAME number — they are two views of one value.

### Why it is shaped this way

The hero is the only card an AE is guaranteed to read, so it carries the whole argument in one screen: the composite, the four pillar scores with their peer deltas, the posture label, and — measured on this render — a FIRMOGRAPHICS strip. For the client it must be recognisably them; for the AE it must supply the one number that frames the meeting and the named capability behind it.

What the render establishes: the composite is 3.4, the posture chip reads COMPETING, provenance reads EVIDENCE · HYBRID, and the framing line is "Trails regional bank peer median by -0.3 points. Gap concentrated in P4 Data foundation." That last sentence is the model for this card. It is not a score read-out — it states the gap, quantifies it, and localises it. A hero that says "overall maturity 3.4" has told the AE nothing they cannot see.

Peer scores are frequently absent, and this is where fabrication happens. The sanctioned answers, in order, are: recompute the median at a lower N by dropping the peer that lacks the figure (floor N=3; N=5 -> sorted[2], N=4 -> mean of sorted[1..2], N=3 -> sorted[1]); then adjacency inference from a neighbouring sub-capability, labelled INFERENCE with its reasoning; then a proxy ceiling (a LinkedIn specialist ratio under 5% caps P1C4 at L2.5, negative-dominant Glassdoor caps at L3.0, lowest cap wins); then stop and print "Cannot reliably estimate". Never impute a value into the cell. The report must disclose proxying with the literal phrase "peer proxy", because a governance check greps for it, and must never claim "identical methodology".

The grain rule owns this card. A quoted number and the cell id it names must be the same cell. One line that paired a sub-capability's score with a category's id produced 125 gate violations across the corpus; it fell to 40, then 8, then 1 as each layer was fixed and re-measured, and none of it was accommodated by raising a ceiling.

### Information sources

Field / element | Source of truth | Where it comes from

overall_score | Scoring workbook | mean of the four pillar means, 2dp; never a flat mean of subcaps (those weight pillars by catalogue size)

pillar_scores[] | Scoring workbook | runs.workbook_scores.pillars, else the rollup of subcap_scores by LEFT(subcap_id,2)

peer_median | peer_comparison_table.csv | per-pillar cohort median

band | lib/maturity.ts | single source of truth for score→band→hex (ADR 0008)

## O2 · Firmographics strip

Payload section overview.firmographics · component ScoreRing inline strip

Contract. Assets, employees and branches rendered inside the hero rather than as a separate panel. Every figure identity-checked against the entity.

### What must be presented

- Employees, revenue, AUM or assets, CAGR, HQ, branches, founded year, primary regulator, charter — each as value + provenance.
- Every populated field shows where it came from; an unknown field renders an em dash, never a guess and never a dict repr.
- Figures must be about THIS legal entity. A parent, subsidiary or same-name institution is a contamination, and the panel is quarantined rather than shown.
- Magnitude sanity: an AUM or asset figure implying a market-scale absurdity is rejected (a $2.70T AUM on a mid-market manager was a real defect).

Two things about this card must be stated plainly. First, the prototype has NO dedicated firmographics panel: the data renders as a FIRMOGRAPHICS strip inside the hero (measured labels: Assets, Employees, Branches) plus the Financial trajectory card. The specification is written to that reality.

Second, this render contains a live identity-contamination defect, and it is the best available worked example of why this card needs a gate. Measured on one client, Zions Bancorporation, on one run:

the Overview Financial trajectory card reads "$12.2B assets · FY2025", with a

## O3 · Why-now signals

Payload section overview.why_now · component WhyNowStrip

Contract. Dated, cited events. Each carries trigger, window, consequence of waiting, cost of acting now and why this sequence.

### What must be presented

- Three to six trigger cards, each a DATED external event with a kind pill, a one-line body and its E-ID.
- Each signal answers 'why call them now' — a leadership change, an earnings move, a regulatory action, a technology announcement, an M&A event.
- A signal is an EVENT, not a score read-out. 'P2 scores 2.4' is not a why-now.
- No signal may be the assessment itself ('Zennify completed a Digital Maturity Assessment' shipped on 11 clients and is circular).

### Why it is shaped this way

A why-now is not a list of changes. It is the synthesis of the whole report, answering one question: why should this entity act NOW rather than next year. Changes are the raw material; the synthesis is the product.

Measured on this render the card is "Why now signals · 3 triggers · click any signal to drill into the evidence", and the drilldown returns reasoning of the form "Closing P1C1.6.4 lifts the parent category and unblocks downstream work." That is the right shape — a trigger tied to a cell, tied to a consequence.

Two things the review identified as missing, both of which are now required.

First, the card must be SEGMENTED, and each header has an ideal: - TRIGGER — what changed, dated, cited. Ideal: a dated external event with a regulator, a filing, a vendor milestone or a named hire behind it. Not "digital transformation is accelerating". - WINDOW — how long the opening lasts, and what closes it. Ideal: a specific closing event with a date ("window closes at nCino go-live, Q2 2026"), because a window with no closing condition is not a window. - CONSEQUENCE OF WAITING — which assessed capability degrades, and by when. Ideal: named cell, direction, horizon. - COST OF ACTING NOW — the honest other side. Ideal: the concurrent commitment this would collide with, drawn from the timeline and the issue register — a live core migration, an open consent order, an integration already tracking to Q3. If acting now is genuinely cheap, say why, but say it. - WHY THIS SEQUENCE — why this is first rather than second, tied to the roadmap.

### Information sources

Field / element | Source of truth | Where it comes from

signals[].body | Research workbook + enrichment | dated timeline events within a 24-month lookback, most recent first

signals[].kind | producer classification | LEADERSHIP │ EARNINGS │ REGULATORY │ TECHNOLOGY │ M&A │ MARKET

signals[].event_date | the source | required; an undated signal is dropped

signals[].e_ids | evidence store | at least one; uncited signals are dropped

## O4 · Executive summary

Payload section overview.exec_summary · component SCQACard

Contract. Situation, complication, question, answer. At most one raw score, and client facts must outnumber score references.

### What must be presented

- A Situation–Complication–Question–Answer narrative, 4 short paragraphs, that an AE can read aloud.
- At most ONE raw score in the whole summary; the story carries the argument, not the arithmetic. 131/138 bodies once quoted two or more.
- Every factual claim carries an E-ID. Client facts must outnumber score references.
- Any score quoted must resolve to a served cell under the label used — quoting a category average under a subcapability's name is the O1/S23 grain defect.

### Why it is shaped this way

Measured, this card is "Executive narrative · SCQA · Assessment Report · stored verbatim · Read full ↓". The review's objection stands: a summary that recaps scores is not a summary. The scores are already on the same screen, twice.

An executive summary earns its place by doing four things the rest of the page cannot. It synthesises the CORPUS rather than the scorecard — the report, the evidence, the issue register, the sentiment, the stack. It is DATA-LED, opening on the fact that most changes the reader's mind. It names the RECOMMENDATIONS and, critically, JUSTIFIES THEIR SEQUENCING, because sequencing is the one judgement the client cannot make from the heatmap. And it is one story that survives contact with the other five pages.

SCQA is the structure, and each element has a job: - SITUATION — the client's own position in their own terms, recognisable to them, with a figure that is theirs. - COMPLICATION — the constraint. This is where the analysis lives. It is a mechanism, not a low score: "applications abandon at identity verification, so the branch channel is absorbing volume the digital channel was built to take" is a complication; "onboarding scores 2.1" is a measurement. - QUESTION — the decision the client faces, phrased as they would phrase it. - ANSWER — the sequenced recommendation, with the reason the sequence is that way, and what it costs to wait.

The safeguard against score-recapping has to be mechanical, because prose drifts: no sentence may consist of a score predicate, at most one numeric score may appear in the whole summary, and the Complication must contain a causal connective. A corpus sweep found 219 findings across 83 clients opening with "stands at 2.34/5", which is what happens without a mechanical rule.

### Information sources

Field / element | Source of truth | Where it comes from

scqa_md | Assessment report DOCX | executive_summary section, then the per-pillar deep dives for the Complication

cited_e_ids | research workbook | every claim's E-ID, validated against this run's evidence store

scores quoted | scoring workbook | must match the served cell ±0.05 at the grain named

A summary written from the scores alone is the failure mode this card exists to avoid. {situation, complication, question, answer, sequencing_rationale,  cost_of_delay, e_ids[], claim_label}   situation     50-90 words. ## O5 · Opportunity surface tiles

Payload section overview.opportunity · component OpportunitySurfaceStrip

Contract. Where engagement value concentrates per platform, tied to the cells below M3 that the platform addresses.

### What must be presented

- Five tiles naming where value is available, each with a headline, a one-line rationale and its anchor capability.
- Prose must be whole sentences — tiles once shipped head-clipped mid-word.
- Each tile names a capability in words and ties to a platform the client could use.

### Why it is shaped this way

Measured: "Opportunity Surface · per platform · Composite fit score 0-100 · Σ(priority × gap) for ABS", and clicking a tile NAVIGATES to #/clients/<id>/platform?platform=DB — it is a cross-page link into the Platform page scoped to that platform, not a panel.

The review's objection is that these tiles are shallow: a composite number with no visible reasoning and no validation. A fit score is a claim about what the client should buy. It is the most commercially loaded number in the product and it must be the most defensible.

Three requirements follow. The composite must be DECOMPOSABLE — the tile shows Σ(priority × gap) and the drilldown must reproduce exactly that arithmetic from the cells it names. A tile showing 47/100 whose expansion recomputes to something else is the defect class that hit 570 of 685 cards; the fix is to rebake the factors after any override rather than render two numbers from two code paths.

It must be VALIDATED against the assessment report, not just the score matrix. The report says what the analyst concluded; if the arithmetic ranks a platform the report never discusses, that disagreement is a finding about one of them.

### Information sources

Field / element | Source of truth | Where it comes from

tiles[].headline | Assessment report + recommendations_detail.json | the gap framed as available value

tiles[].anchor_subcap_id | scoring workbook | the subcapability the tile is about

tiles[].platform | platform fit engine v2 | deterministic; the agent explains, never recomputes

### Synthesis prompt

```
Produce the opportunity tiles: composite fit per platform, decomposable and validated. STEP 1 - COMPUTE, SHOWING THE PARTS composite = Σ(priority × gap) over the addressable cells, normalised 0-100. Emit {platform, composite, factors[], addressable_cells[], relevance, their_stack_context, rank, rank_rationale}   factors[]          {name, weight, value, contribution} and they MUST sum to                      the composite. The drilldown reproduces this arithmetic.   addressable_cells[] {subcap_id, name, current, peer, gap, feature_that                       _addresses_it}. Every cell must be one THIS run serves.   relevance          0-1, how relevant this platform is to THIS sub-vertical. STEP 2 - DISCARD (a ranking that cannot reject is a sort) Drop a platform when: relevance < 0.5 for the sub-vertical; the anchor cells belong to a different entity type (a carrier sub-capability on a bank); the client already runs it at the layer in question (that is an adoption conversation, not a fit conversation); or it addresses fewer than 3 cells. Emit discarded[] with {platform, reason} - a visible discard is evidence of judgement, and it is what an AE needs when the client asks "why not X". STEP 3 - FACTOR IN THEIR OWN STACK  (this changes the answer, not the framing) Read the tech stack register. A CONFIRMED platform at a layer removes that layer's greenfield opportunity and creates an extension one. An ABSENT platform with a demand signal (hiring, an RFP, a board commitment) RAISES priority. D Probes: out-of-vertical rank-1; anchor-cell entity-type mismatch; stale    fit figure (an exec_fit computed against a superseded run);    Tech Stack Mismatch (recommending what they already own). STEP 6 - RANK RATIONALE  (25-45 words per tile) Why this platform sits at this rank, naming the cells it addresses and the constraint it lifts. Not a restatement of the composite. GATES: S13_platform_score_lead; S17_exec_fit_stale; S31_platform _distinctiveness; breakdown-equals-headline.

## O6 · Top findings

Payload section overview.findings · component TopFindingsCard

Contract. Ranked findings, each with what, why and so what, a named consequence, a rejected alternative, and an inline expansion.

### What must be presented

- Four to six findings, each a capability-named headline with a What/Why/So-what body and evidence chips.
- The score chip and the anchor id must name the SAME cell. A subcap-grain score under a category id read '3.5/5' against a cell serving 2.77 on 59 clients.

# Insights — surface contracts

A triage surface, not a grid. Cards group by priority, pillar or theme; the priority is a commercial judgement the card carries rather than a filter the reader applies. The technology landscape strip sits here because the gap it names is what makes an insight actionable.

## I1 · Insight cards

Payload section insights.insights · component ClientInsights

Contract. Triage layout grouped by priority, pillar or theme. Each card carries a claim as its title and opens a four-tab modal.

### What must be presented

- Six to ten cards, each a defensible argument: what, why it matters, what to do, with severity and the capability it anchors on.
- Zero cards on a completed run is a failure state, not an empty state.
- No card may open with a score read-out; the what_text is a claim, not a metric.
- Every linked_subcap_id must resolve to a served cell — dead links were 15 of 119.

### Why it is shaped this way

The review asked directly: what really IS an insight card, what makes it useful, and what are the non-negotiables. The answer the rest of this section is built on:

An insight card is a DEFENSIBLE ARGUMENT THAT CHANGES WHAT SOMEONE DOES. It has five parts — a claim, the evidence for it, the mechanism connecting them, a competing explanation considered and rejected, and the decision it implies. Remove any one and it stops being an insight.

Observation: "Onboarding scores 2.1." Insight: "Onboarding is the constraint on deposit growth, because applications abandon at identity verification, which is why the branch channel is absorbing volume the digital channel was built to take."

The observation gives a number the reader could have read off the heatmap. The insight gives a causal chain they could not. The reader's response to the first is "noted"; to the second, "then we fix IDV before we market the app".

cards[].e_ids | research workbook | mandatory; the serve layer excludes uncited cards

## H3 · Thin-evidence alerts

Payload section heatmap.alerts · component ClientHealth alerts tab

Contract. The run's under-evidenced cells with severity, current count, proxy attempted and closure condition.

### What must be presented

- One alert per cell scored on insufficient evidence, with severity and the cell it concerns, feeding the Alerts queue.
- The register is payload-produced; the legacy deriver is switched off.

### Why it is shaped this way

Measured, the Health page carries five toggle views — Thin-evidence alerts, Version diff, Safeguard gates, Evidence age and Cross-entity patterns — and the alerts table runs to 13,713px for 252 open items on this client.

The review's point is the whole design principle for this surface: the goal is not to raise alerts but to enrich and justify. 252 open alerts is not 252 pieces of information; it is a queue that has not been worked. An alert that has been open across three runs with no enrichment attempt is evidence about our process, not about the client.

So the alert is a work item with a lifecycle, not a label. Every alert names the cell, what was searched, what was found, and what it would take to close. And the surface must distinguish two genuinely different states that look identical in a count: cells that are thin because nobody looked, and cells that are thin because the evidence does not exist. The first is a backlog; the second is a finding, and it belongs in the narrative.

### Information sources

Field / element | Source of truth | Where it comes from

alerts[] | scoring workbook is_thin_evidence + link reconcile | cleared only by real linked support, never set by the producer

severity | producer | mapped from the evidence deficit

```

## H5 · Safeguard gates

Payload section heatmap.safeguard_gates · component ClientHealth gates tab

Contract. Client-visible gate results in plain language, including an explicit not-run state with its reason.

### What must be presented

- The controls the assessment applied — caps enforced, uncertainty bands, QA verdicts — shown so a reader can see what constrained the scores.
- This section had zero rows and no producer before it was contracted.

### Why it is shaped this way

Safeguard gates are the client-visible expression of our own discipline, which makes honesty here structural rather than optional. A gate that reports PASS because it did not run is worse than a gate that reports FAIL.

Each gate states what it checked, what it found, and what a failure would mean for the reader's confidence in the surrounding numbers. The three classes behave differently and must be labelled as such: BLOCK gates (identity, regulatory anchor, traceability, classification anchors, no toolkit blending) stop publication; CONFIDENCE gates reduce the confidence band without stopping it; LABELLING gates force a relabel - a trend with fewer than three dated points becomes a snapshot rather than disappearing.

Page operation

# How each page operates

Every page follows the same skeleton — resolve, fetch, redact, render, interact — and each diverges from it in ways that matter. Below is the operational sequence for all seven dashboards.

The shared invariants
Every page is server-rendered from serving tables in a single round trip. Every drilldown payload ships inline on that response, so no panel fetches. Redaction happens at serialisation, so the browser never receives an internal field. Ordering is payload ordering. And no page computes a colour, a score, a delta or an age — all four are either promoted or generated by the database.

## D1 · Overview · 12 sections · 13 surfaces

2

Fetch
Twelve concurrent selects, one per section. A section with no promoted row returns its empty state rather than a null-filled object.

3

Redact
Every JSON path in each section's internal-only array is deleted before serialisation. For the customer audience, O1b, O9 and O12 are withheld whole.

4

Render
Server-rendered in one pass. The hero first — it is the only card guaranteed to be read — then the strip, then the instrumentation.

5

Reconcile
The hero composite is asserted equal to the heatmap rollup and to the run-history score at read time; a mismatch renders a quarantine note rather than a number.

6

Interact

## Failure behaviour, per page

Condition | Behaviour

No promoted run for the entity | The client shell renders with an awaiting-synthesis state. No tab shows a partial page.

A section has no promoted row | That surface renders its empty state with a data-source of empty. The rest of the page renders normally.

A section promoted with an explicit empty state | The stated reason renders, with what was searched. This is a value, not a failure.

A figure was quarantined at synthesis | A dash with the provenance note. Never a substituted value.

A cited evidence id does not resolve | Cannot occur — submission would have failed. If it somehow does, the chip is not rendered at all rather than rendered dead.

A run is promoted while a page is open | The page stays pinned to the run it loaded. A banner offers the newer run. Numbers never change under an open page.

A role loses access mid-session | The next API call refuses; the route redirects to the directory with a stated reason.

Versioning

# Versioning across assessments

Four independent version axes. Conflating any two of them produces a comparison that looks valid and is not.

Axis | Carried by | Owned by | What it means

Run | run_id · run_seq | The app, at ingest | One complete assessment of one entity at one point in time.

Catalogue | ccg_catalog_version | The catalogue loader | Which capability catalogue the run was scored against.

Producer | producer_version | The synthesis agent, per submission | Which synthesis produced each section.

Contract | contract_version | The connector, per section | The payload shape the submission validated against.

## Run version

One complete assessment of one entity at one point in time.

Promotion swaps which run is ACTIVE. Prior runs stay queryable forever — the longitudinal surfaces read across them.

## Catalogue version

Which capability catalogue the run was scored against.

Pinned at ingest and never changed for that run. Cross-run comparison is only valid within a lineage; across a bump every cell resolves through the alias bridge or is marked not-comparable.

## Producer version

Which synthesis produced each section.

Stamped on every serving row. Lets one page be re-synthesised and re-promoted without re-running the assessment, and lets a regression be bisected to a producer.

## Contract version

The payload shape the submission validated against.

Recorded on the staged row. A contract bump invalidates staged-but-unpromoted work for the changed sections only; promoted content is unaffected until re-synthesised.

## Run lineage

```
entity: shore-united-bank
  │
  ├─ run 1   req DMA-ASM-SUB-20251104-0001   catalogue v7.0   SUPERSEDED
  │            composite 2.9 · scored_cells 742
  │
  ├─ run 2   req DMA-ASM-SUB-20260317-0002   catalogue v7.0   SUPERSEDED
  │            composite 3.1 · scored_cells 748
  │            comparable to run 1 cell-by-cell — same catalogue
  │
  └─ run 3   req DMA-ASM-SUB-20260731-0003   catalogue v7.1   ACTIVE
               composite 3.4 · scored_cells 761
               comparison to runs 1–2 must resolve through the alias bridge;
               cells with no bridge are marked NOT_COMPARABLE, not omitted
```

## What a version diff may and may not assert

Comparison | Permitted

Two runs, same catalogue version | Cell-by-cell. Score deltas, coverage deltas, evidence-count deltas, recommendation carry-over.

Two runs, catalogue bump, cell bridged by an alias | Cell-by-cell, with the rename disclosed. The diff names both ids.

Two runs, catalogue bump, cell split or merged | Category level only. The cell row renders NOT_COMPARABLE with the reason.

Two runs, catalogue bump, cell retired | Not comparable. Rendered as retired, never as a drop to zero.

Composite across a catalogue bump | Disclosed as approximate. The denominator changed, so the composite is not strictly the same measurement.

Any comparison across entities | Never on a client surface. Cross-entity work is counts and shares only, on Health.

The failure this prevents
A renamed cell compared naively across a catalogue bump reads as a capability that vanished — which looks like a regression, gets investigated as one, and is actually a rename. A split cell compared naively reads as a score that halved. Both are the diff asserting more than the data supports, and both are prevented by refusing the comparison rather than by annotating it afterwards.

## Re-synthesising one page without a new run

A section can be wrong without the assessment being wrong. The producer version axis exists so that case does not require a rerun.

```
1  The run is already PROMOTED. Its staging rows are retained, marked promoted.
2  A Cowork session re-claims the run.
3  It resubmits ONE page. That supersedes the live staging row for that page
   and carries a new producer_version.
4  promote_run runs again. It asserts a PASS on every page — five come from the
   retained staging rows, one from the new submission — and rewrites all
   34 serving tables in one transaction.
5  Rows from the five unchanged pages are rewritten byte-identically but with a
   new promoted_at. Rows from the changed page carry the new producer_version.

The atomic guarantee is unaffected: promotion is still all-or-nothing
across all 6 pages. What changes is that four of them did not need
re-synthesising, only re-promoting.
```

This is why promoted staging rows are retained rather than deleted. If promotion cleared its inputs, fixing one card would mean re-synthesising all 6 pages — which in practice means nobody fixes one card.

## Catalogue bumps

Event | Effect

A new catalogue version is loaded | Rows are added, keyed on the new version. Nothing existing is mutated.

An existing run is opened | Resolves against the version it was pinned to. Unaffected.

A new run is ingested | Pins to the current version.

A cell was renamed | The alias bridge resolves it. Drilldowns keep working; the diff discloses the rename.

A cell was split, merged or retired | Recorded in the bridge with its reason. Comparison degrades to the nearest comparable grain and says so.

The toggle cascade changed for a sub-vertical | Affects new runs only. An existing run's scored-cell count is stored, not recomputed.

Persistence

# Runs, payloads, users and sessions

Five classes of state with five different owners and five different lifetimes. The most common design error here is treating content as user state, or view state as server state.

Class | Owner | Holds | Lives in

Content | Global — identical for every user | Promoted section rows, evidence, scores, gates | Serving tables, keyed by run

Synthesis session | Owned by one Cowork session at a time | The claim lease, staged submissions, verdicts | Staging tables + a lease in the cache

User workspace | Per user, per entity | Annotations, alert dispositions, last-viewed run, saved searches | Workflow tables, keyed by user and entity

View state | Per user, per tab — ephemeral but shareable | Audience, heatmap view mode and grain, open panel, filters, Health tab | The URL

Pinned run | Per user, per entity | Which run the user is currently reading | URL parameter, defaulted from the workspace row

## Content

Global — identical for every user · Promoted section rows, evidence, scores, gates

Never per-user. Two AEs opening the same client see the same numbers. Only the audience filter differs, and audience is a request parameter, not a user attribute.

## Synthesis session

Owned by one Cowork session at a time · The claim lease, staged submissions, verdicts

Staged work outlives the session. The lease expires so a dead session cannot block a run; a new session resumes from run progress.

## User workspace

Per user, per entity · Annotations, alert dispositions, last-viewed run, saved searches

Survives sign-out. Never presents as synthesis output — an annotation is attributed and dated.

## View state

Per user, per tab — ephemeral but shareable · Audience, heatmap view mode and grain, open panel, filters, Health tab

Nothing server-side. A URL is the unit of sharing: paste it to a colleague and they see exactly your view, subject to their own role.

## Pinned run

Per user, per entity · Which run the user is currently reading

Set on first load so a promotion mid-session cannot silently swap the numbers under an open page.

## Two AEs, one client

```
AE-1 opens /clients/shore-united-bank/overview
AE-2 opens /clients/shore-united-bank/overview        same moment

Both resolve to run 3 (ACTIVE) and read the same 12 serving rows.
Both see IDENTICAL numbers, prose and citations.

What can differ:
  · audience          a URL parameter, not a user attribute
  · pinned run        AE-1 may still be reading run 2 from a shared link
  · view state        grain, filters, open panel — all in the URL
  · annotations       each sees their own AND their colleagues', attributed
  · role              an AE and an analyst see different tab sets

Content is never per-user. There is no per-user copy of a payload,
no per-user cache, and nothing to reconcile between two readers.
```

## A promotion lands while a page is open

```
t0   AE opens /clients/x/overview          → resolves run 2, pins ?run=<run2>
t1   Cowork promotes run 3                  → run 2 becomes SUPERSEDED
t2   AE clicks the Heatmap tab              → still ?run=<run2>, still run 2
t3   A banner appears: "A newer assessment is available."
t4   AE clicks through                      → ?run=<run3>, whole shell reloads

The numbers never change under an open page. An AE who quoted a figure
at t0 can still see that figure at t2. Silent mid-session substitution is
the one behaviour this design refuses outright.
```

Why pinning is not optional
An AE reads the hero, walks into a meeting, and refers back to the tab twenty minutes later. If the run swapped in between, the figure they quoted is no longer on screen and they cannot tell whether they misread it or the data moved. Pinning costs one URL parameter and removes the entire class.

## A Cowork session dies mid-synthesis

```
t0   session A claims run 3, lease expires at t0+2h
t1   session A submits overview → PASS, STAGED
t2   session A submits insights → FAIL, 3 reasons
t3   session A dies

     Staged work persists. The overview submission is untouched.
     The verdict persists. Including the failing one.

t4   lease expires. The run returns to unclaimed.
t5   session B claims run 3 and calls get_run_progress:
       overview  pass       insights  fail (3 reasons)
       heatmap   missing    platform  missing
       context   missing    techstack missing
       promotable: false
t6   session B reads the three reasons, repairs, resubmits insights.
     It never re-synthesises the overview.
```

## Concurrency rules

Situation | Behaviour

Two Cowork sessions, same run | The second is refused. The lease is exclusive.

Two Cowork sessions, different runs of one entity | Allowed. Claims are per-run. Whichever promotes last becomes active.

Two Cowork sessions, different entities | Allowed and expected. This is the normal throughput case.

A session resubmits a page it already passed | Allowed. The new row supersedes; the old verdict is retained.

A session calls promote twice | Idempotent. The second reaches the same state.

Promotion races an ingest of a newer run | Both persist. Active is decided by promotion order, not ingest order.

An AE annotates a run that is then superseded | The annotation stays attached to the run it was made against, and is visible in that run's history rather than migrating.

An alert is resolved, then a rerun reopens the same cell | A new alert row. The prior disposition is history, and the reopening is itself a signal — the closure condition did not hold.

## What survives what

Event | Survives | Does not survive

A user signs out | Annotations, alert dispositions, last-viewed run, saved searches | View state — unless the URL was saved

A Cowork session ends | Staged submissions, verdicts, registered evidence | The claim lease, once expired

A run is superseded | Everything. Scores, evidence, sections, verdicts, annotations, alerts | Its ACTIVE status only

A catalogue bump | Every existing run, resolving against its pinned version | Naive cross-version cell comparison

A contract change | Promoted content, until re-synthesised | Staged-but-unpromoted work for the changed sections

A re-promotion | The run, its history and its annotations | The previous promoted_at on rewritten rows

## Retention

Deliberately an operational policy rather than a contract. The one constraint the contract does impose: a superseded run must remain queryable long enough for the longitudinal surfaces — velocity, recurring themes, the seen-in-N-runs chip and version diff — to read across it. In practice that means every run for an entity that is still an active account.

Rendering

# Card anatomy and colour

Every surface renders into a fixed shell. Prose written without knowing where it lands is prose that clips mid-word — a measured defect on 501 cards.

## The universal card shell

```
┌─ card ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  card-head                                                        │
│    <h3>            CARD HEADER   2–4 words, sentence case         │
│    badge           COUNT or STATE   right-aligned                 │
├───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  rows / body                                                      │
│    chip            ID CHIP       the item's own id, monospace     │
│    row title       13px / 600 / line-height 1.35   ≤ 12 words     │
│    eyebrow line    10px / uppercase / letter-spacing .05em        │
│                    THEME · magnitude · platform chips             │
│    chevron         expand affordance, where the row expands       │
├───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  expansion         inset panel, appears in flow, no overlay       │
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The header names the card, never the finding. The row title carries the claim. The eyebrow carries orientation — theme, then magnitude, then platform. The expansion carries the argument. A row title that needs the eyebrow to make sense is too short; an eyebrow that repeats the title is wasted.

## Slot budgets, per surface

Surface | Slot | Content | Budget

O1 | Framing line | The gap, quantified and localised. Must not open with the composite — it renders beside the number | 18–32 words

O1 | Ring label | The band word | 1 word

O3 | Five headers | Trigger · Window · Consequence of waiting · Cost of acting now · Why this sequence | all required

O6 | Row title | A claim, ideally one that rejects the obvious alternative | ≤ 12 words

O6 | Eyebrow | THEME, then quantified magnitude | 1–3 · 6–14 words

O6 | Expansion | what · why · so what · rejected alternative | 55–95 words

I1 | WHAT | What is observably true, cited | 30–60 words

I1 | WHY | The mechanism — not a restatement of the what | 30–60 words

I1 | SO WHAT | The commercial consequence, one clause | 15–30 words

I1 | Rejected alternative | The competing explanation, and why the evidence favours yours | 20–35 words

H2 | Synthesis | With the grounded-on count printed beside it | 40–90 words

P1 | Platform story | What it changes · which constraint it lifts · what it depends on · what it does not solve | 90–150 words

P2b | Starter text | Say-it-aloud. No codes, no bracketed ids, no score-first opening | 45–90 words

P3 | Phase rationale | Why this phase sits here and not earlier | 30–60 words

H5 | Plain label | A human sentence beside every client-visible gate | 8–18 words

C2 | Issue title | The matter in the source's own terms | 8–16 words

The insight modal's sub-headings are literal and uppercase in the prototype — WHAT, WHY, SO WHAT — alongside the ACTIONED, CRITICAL and OPPORTUNITY badges. The opportunity framing is native to the interface, not an editorial overlay.

## Colour: the payload carries none

Score resolves to band resolves to hex in one module. No payload field carries a hex value, a class name or an outline instruction. The payload carries the raw score, the band word where a surface renders one, and semantic flags — thin evidence, below threshold, primary gap.

Band | Word | Boundary | Fill

M1 | Activating | score < 2.0 | #FFCB99

M2 | Building | 2.0 ≤ score < 3.0 | #62D7B8

M3 | Competing | 3.0 ≤ score < 4.0 | #27BBAF

M4 | Differentiating | score ≥ 4.0 | #139F94

— | No score | score is null | #E5E7EB

Boundaries are strict less-than on the raw score, before display rounding. A score of 2.97 displays as 3.0 and bands as Building — the band comes from the raw value and the label from the rounded one, and they diverge at the boundary.

Three conflicts worth knowing before writing a band word
Transformational does not render — the resolver has four branches and anything at or above 4.0 returns Differentiating. The M2 hex disagrees between sources — #62D7B8 in the resolver, #B0EDD3 in the published palette; the resolver wins because it is what renders. And the word "stale" carries two meanings — over 12 months on the freshness dot, 36 to 48 months on the evidence ladder. The evidence ladder governs anything the payload emits.

Describe the number, not the colour. "Data foundation sits at 1.9" is checkable. "Data foundation is red" depends on a palette the writer does not control and cannot see. Thin evidence adds a dashed outline and does not change the fill, because the fill means maturity and nothing else.
