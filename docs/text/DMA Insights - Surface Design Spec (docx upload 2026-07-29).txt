0. Mandate for the executing agent
This document is the design contract for every surface DMA Insights serves. The remediation prompt it accompanies was defect-driven: it described what was wrong on ten Overview cards and eleven others. This one is surface-driven and complete — it covers every card whether or not it has ever had a defect, so that a card can be built, reviewed or regenerated without reading a defect register first.
The PROTOTYPE is the visual contract. Screenshots here were captured by rendering Standalone_5 in Chromium at 1680px, signed in through the prototype's own quick-in, and walking each surface. They are the intended design, not the live app.
Every card states three things: what must be presented, where each field comes from, and the prompt that produces it. A card with no stated source is not buildable.
Prompts are initial prompts. They are written to be pasted into the Claude Code analysis routine, one section per submission, and they assume the analyst preamble in §11.1 is prepended.
Nothing on any surface may be invented. Every number traces to a workbook cell or a parsed artefact; every claim carries an E-ID. Where a source is genuinely absent the card renders an honest empty state — it does not fill the space.
The app never calls a model. Content arrives as a validated payload over the MCP and is written to the serving tables by promote_run.

2. How the screenshots were produced
So that any reviewer can regenerate them rather than trust them:
Source: DMA_Insights___Standalone_5.html (2,490,184 bytes). The repo's frontend/standalone-src/standalone-original.html is the same artefact at 2,446,560 bytes and is byte-identical to the Drive design package.
The single-file bundle is not greppable for app structure: its 2.33MB line is base64 image data, and the app's own source is not in the file as plain text. So the rendered behaviour is measured by executing it — served over local HTTP and driven with Playwright 1.60 against the pre-installed Chromium at /opt/pw-browsers/chromium-1194.
The authoritative source for WHAT each control opens is the repo, not the bundle: frontend/standalone-src/src/ holds 13 modules, of which drawers.jsx (85KB) defines EvidenceDrawer, InsightModal, IntelligencePanel, NewRunModal and RecommendationModal, pages-d3-heatmap.jsx defines SynthesisDrawer, and pages-d5-d6-tech-runs.jsx defines EventDetail, IssueDetail and ClientTechStackDetail. Read the component to find the trigger, then click the trigger to measure the panel — that order is what turned a zero-drilldown result into 106 captures.
Viewport 1680x1050 at deviceScaleFactor 2, full-page capture. The Alerts surface is 83,130px tall (1,512 rows) and exceeds Chromium's texture limit, so it is clipped to 9,000px — noted in its caption.
The shell navigates by React state, not hash routes, so capture clicks nav and tab controls by label. Client shown throughout: Zions Bancorporation (maturity 3.4).
Where the prototype and the repo source disagree, the PROTOTYPE wins for layout: Standalone_5 is newer than standalone-src and its Insights page is a triage layout (Act now / Plan next / Watch) rather than the flat .ic grid the repo module renders. Section 13 is measured against the prototype throughout.

PROTOTYPE · Scores and peer benchmarks (O1) — also on this surface: Capability ceiling & uncertainty.
What must be presented — Scores and peer benchmarks (4.1 O1)
Overall maturity to 1dp in the hero ring, with its band word (Building, Competitive…).
Four pillar scores P1–P4 as a bar strip, each to 1dp with peer delta arrow.
Peer median per pillar and the cohort the median is drawn from.
Every number must equal the served cell to within one 2dp rounding step. The hero ring and the run row must render the SAME number — they are two views of one value.
Why this is what it is — Scores and peer benchmarks (4.1 O1)
The hero is the only card an AE is guaranteed to read, so it carries the whole argument in one screen: the composite, the four pillar scores with their peer deltas, the posture label, and — measured on this render — a FIRMOGRAPHICS strip. For the client it must be recognisably them; for the AE it must supply the one number that frames the meeting and the named capability behind it.
What the render establishes: the composite is 3.4, the posture chip reads COMPETING, provenance reads EVIDENCE · HYBRID, and the framing line is "Trails regional bank peer median by -0.3 points. Gap concentrated in P4 Data foundation." That last sentence is the model for this card. It is not a score read-out — it states the gap, quantifies it, and localises it. A hero that says "overall maturity 3.4" has told the AE nothing they cannot see.
Peer scores are frequently absent, and this is where fabrication happens. The sanctioned answers, in order, are: recompute the median at a lower N by dropping the peer that lacks the figure (floor N=3; N=5 -> sorted[2], N=4 -> mean of sorted[1..2], N=3 -> sorted[1]); then adjacency inference from a neighbouring sub-capability, labelled INFERENCE with its reasoning; then a proxy ceiling (a LinkedIn specialist ratio under 5% caps P1C4 at L2.5, negative-dominant Glassdoor caps at L3.0, lowest cap wins); then stop and print "Cannot reliably estimate". Never impute a value into the cell. The report must disclose proxying with the literal phrase "peer proxy", because a governance check greps for it, and must never claim "identical methodology".
The grain rule owns this card. A quoted number and the cell id it names must be the same cell. One line that paired a sub-capability's score with a category's id produced 125 gate violations across the corpus; it fell to 40, then 8, then 1 as each layer was fixed and re-measured, and none of it was accommodated by raising a ceiling.

Claude Code prompt — Scores and peer benchmarks (4.1 O1)
Produce the hero: composite, four pillar scores with peer deltas, posture, the
framing sentence, and the firmographics strip.

STEP 1 - READ, DO NOT DERIVE
Scores come from the workbook. You never assign M1-M5. Read the composite as the
mean of the four pillar means (NOT a flat mean of all sub-capabilities - those
differ, and shipping both produced a hero ring and a run row that disagreed at
1dp on 26 clients). Round once, at 2dp, then present at 1dp: rounding to 2dp and
then to 1dp is not the same function as rounding once, and .x5 ties diverge.

STEP 2 - PEER FIGURES, WHERE THE TABLE HAS THEM
Per pillar: {pillar_id, score, peer_median, delta, direction}. delta is signed
and is computed, never restated from the source.

STEP 3 - PEER FIGURES, WHERE IT DOES NOT  (in this order, stop at the first
that yields a defensible number)
  a RECOMPUTE AT LOWER N. Drop the peer lacking the figure; take the median of
    the rest. Floor N=3. N=5 -> sorted[2]; N=4 -> mean(sorted[1..2]);
    N=3 -> sorted[1]. Emit peer_n so the reader knows the basis shrank.
  b ADJACENCY INFERENCE. A neighbouring sub-capability's evidence implies
    something about this one. Label INFERENCE, state the reasoning in one
    clause, widen the band.
  c PROXY CEILING. LinkedIn specialist ratio <5% caps P1C4 at L2.5;
    negative-dominant Glassdoor caps at L3.0. Lowest cap wins.
  d STOP. Accumulated uncertainty over +/-0.8 -> "Cannot reliably estimate".
    A point estimate past the cap is false precision, which is worse than a
    declared unknown.
  Disclose proxying with the literal phrase "peer proxy". NEVER write
  "identical methodology". NEVER impute a value into the peer cell. The peer set
  is immutable once selected: same sub-vertical, +/-50% asset size, same
  regulator jurisdiction, no M&A distortion inside 24 months.

STEP 4 - THE FRAMING SENTENCE  (18-32 words)
State the gap, quantify it, and localise it to a pillar or a named capability.
Model: "Trails regional bank peer median by -0.3 points. Gap concentrated in P4
Data foundation." Do NOT open with the composite - it is rendered beside you.

STEP 5 - POSTURE
LEADING | COMPETING | LAGGING | MIXED, justified against the peer set, with the
evidence basis chip (EVIDENCE / HYBRID / INFERRED) set from what actually
backs the scores.

STEP 6 - GRAIN ASSERTION (blocking)
For every "<label> at N/5" you emit, resolve the label to a served cell and
assert the score matches within +/-0.05. A mismatch is a grain_violation and the
card does not ship. This is the most common defect in this product.

STEP 7 - ENRICHMENT AND CHALLENGE (R-Layer)
 A State the posture claim and its confidence.
 B Search for what would refute it. At least one contradictory query:
   "[Entity] digital transformation criticism OR delay OR failure".
 C Is the posture reasonable for this sub-vertical, size tier and regulator?
 D Probes: grain mismatch; identity contamination (every figure must be THIS
   entity - check the domain on every source); Peer Outlier (a capability wildly
   unlike peers needs its evidence re-verified); staleness.
 E ACCEPT / REJECT / UNCERTAIN. UNCERTAIN ships with the band stated.

4.2 O2 · Firmographics panel
Payload section: firmographics.fields    |    Required: yes

PROTOTYPE · Firmographics panel (O2) as the card renders.
What must be presented — Firmographics panel (4.2 O2)
Employees, revenue, AUM or assets, CAGR, HQ, branches, founded year, primary regulator, charter — each as value + provenance.
Every populated field shows where it came from; an unknown field renders an em dash, never a guess and never a dict repr.
Figures must be about THIS legal entity. A parent, subsidiary or same-name institution is a contamination, and the panel is quarantined rather than shown.
Magnitude sanity: an AUM or asset figure implying a market-scale absurdity is rejected (a $2.70T AUM on a mid-market manager was a real defect).
Why this is what it is — Firmographics panel (4.2 O2)
Two things about this card must be stated plainly. First, the prototype has NO dedicated firmographics panel: the data renders as a FIRMOGRAPHICS strip inside the hero (measured labels: Assets, Employees, Branches) plus the Financial trajectory card. The specification is written to that reality.
Second, this render contains a live identity-contamination defect, and it is the best available worked example of why this card needs a gate. Measured on one client, Zions Bancorporation, on one run:
the hero firmographics strip reads Assets $87.9B
the Overview Financial trajectory card reads "$12.2B assets · FY2025", with a
the Context Financial trajectory card reads $76.6B -> $87.9B, CAGR 3.5%,
Zions Bancorporation is a Utah-headquartered, OCC-regulated bank of roughly $87B. FCA is the Farm Credit Administration and that five-state Northeast footprint is Farm Credit East's. One card on the page is populated from a different entity's profile — and the focus-area drilldown corroborates it by citing FCE_DMA_Client_Profile_FINAL.docx as its source document. Two cards carrying the same metric name disagree by 7x on the same screen.
That is what an identity gate is for, and it is why "did we get a number" is the wrong question. The right questions are: is this number about THIS entity, is it the most recent one available, and does every other surface that carries it agree. A single wrong figure costs the whole document its credibility with the client, and this one would be caught by the client in the first thirty seconds.
Which firmographics matter is not constant. They are set by sub-vertical, and tiering on the wrong metric is its own error: size tiers are dollar-denominated and exist only for SV1/SV2, which is incoherent for AUM- and premium-based sub-verticals. Where a tier is undefined, say so.

Claude Code prompt — Firmographics panel (4.2 O2)
Produce the firmographics strip. Sub-vertical decides WHICH fields, not just
what values.

STEP 0 - CLASSIFY, THEN CHOOSE THE FIELDS
Classification is regulator first, operating model second, revenue mix as
tiebreak. Never by size, never by product names. First match wins; unmatched
escalates rather than force-fitting.
  SV1 Regional Banks $1B-$100B  total assets, deposits, loans, NIM, efficiency
                                ratio, NPL ratio
  SV2 Credit Unions             total assets, SHARES, loans, net worth ratio,
                                ROA, MEMBER COUNT
  SV3 Commercial Lending        loan portfolio, CRE concentration, C&I volume,
                                NPA ratio
  SV4 CIB / Capital Markets     revenue by segment, trading revenue, IB fees,
                                AUM
  SV5 RIAs & Broker-Dealers     AUM, client count, revenue, ADVISOR COUNT
  SV6 Asset Management          AUM BY STRATEGY, fund performance, NET FLOWS,
                                expense ratios
  SV7 Insurance Brokers         premium placed, commission revenue, PRODUCER
                                COUNT, acquisitions
  SV8 Insurance Carriers        DWP, COMBINED RATIO, LOSS RATIO, investment
                                income, surplus
  Farm Credit                   UNDEFINED in research. Do NOT borrow SV1's
                                metrics. Emit sub_vertical_undefined=true and
                                say so on the surface.
Rendering "shares" for a bank or "deposits" for an RIA is a category error even
when the number is right.

STEP 1 - IDENTITY GATE (blocking, runs before any figure is accepted)
For EVERY field: assert the source document is about THIS legal entity.
  - match the legal name, not the trading name; resolve suffixes
  - check the REGULATOR against the entity's own: an FCA figure on an
    OCC-regulated bank is a different institution
  - check the FOOTPRINT: a five-state Northeast footprint on a Utah bank is a
    different institution
  - check the ORDER OF MAGNITUDE against any other figure for the same metric
    already on the pack. Two figures for one metric that differ by more than
    25% are a contradiction to resolve, not two data points to render.
  Any failure -> QUARANTINE the field. Emit it as absent with
  quarantine_reason, never as a value. A measured case: one client shipped
  $12.2B assets / FCA / NY-NJ-CT-MA-NH on the Overview while the hero and the
  Context page both said $87.9B / OCC. Both cards rendered. That must fail.

STEP 2 - RECENCY GATE (blocking)
Every figure carries {value, as_of, source_e_id, recency_band}. No as_of, no
render. Bands: CURRENT <18mo · RECENT 18-36mo · LEGACY >36mo · UNVERIFIED
undated. A LEGACY figure may render ONLY with its date visible. An UNVERIFIED
figure never renders as current. Report undated_pct for the panel.

STEP 3 - ENRICHMENT (mandatory, not a fallback)
Always search for a NEWER figure than the package holds, because the package is
as old as the assessment:
  - the authoritative registry for the sub-vertical: FDIC BankFind (banks),
    NCUA Research (credit unions), OCC Bank Search, FFIEC NPW, SEC EDGAR
    (10-K/10-Q), SEC IAPD + FINRA BrokerCheck (advisers/BDs), NAIC and AM Best
    (insurers)
  - the entity's own site: MANDATORY fetch - about page, newsroom, investor
    relations, latest quarterly release
  - LinkedIn for headcount; the careers page for footprint
  - the newest quarterly filing, not the annual, when the metric is quarterly
Mint E-CC ids with url + verbatim excerpt + retrieval date + tier + claim label.
If enrichment finds a newer figure that disagrees with the package, the NEWER
specific source wins (recent>older, specific>general) and you emit the
contradiction row rather than silently replacing.

STEP 4 - MAGNITUDE SANITY
Compare against the sub-vertical's plausible range and against the entity's own
prior-year figure. A regional bank at $2.70T is not a large regional bank, it is
a parse error - one client shipped exactly that. Implausible -> quarantine, do
not clamp.

STEP 5 - EMIT
Per field: {field, value, unit, as_of, recency_band, source_e_id,
confidence, quarantined, quarantine_reason}. Absent beats wrong: a dash with a
provenance note is honest; a plausible-looking wrong number is not.

4.3 O3 · Why-now signals
Payload section: why_now.signals    |    Required: yes

PROTOTYPE · Why-now signals (O3) as the card renders.
What must be presented — Why-now signals (4.3 O3)
Three to six trigger cards, each a DATED external event with a kind pill, a one-line body and its E-ID.
Each signal answers 'why call them now' — a leadership change, an earnings move, a regulatory action, a technology announcement, an M&A event.
A signal is an EVENT, not a score read-out. 'P2 scores 2.4' is not a why-now.
No signal may be the assessment itself ('Zennify completed a Digital Maturity Assessment' shipped on 11 clients and is circular).
Why this is what it is — Why-now signals (4.3 O3)
A why-now is not a list of changes. It is the synthesis of the whole report, answering one question: why should this entity act NOW rather than next year. Changes are the raw material; the synthesis is the product.
Measured on this render the card is "Why now signals · 3 triggers · click any signal to drill into the evidence", and the drilldown returns reasoning of the form "Closing P1C1.6.4 lifts the parent category and unblocks downstream work." That is the right shape — a trigger tied to a cell, tied to a consequence.
Two things the review identified as missing, both of which are now required.
First, the card must be SEGMENTED, and each header has an ideal: - TRIGGER — what changed, dated, cited. Ideal: a dated external event with a regulator, a filing, a vendor milestone or a named hire behind it. Not "digital transformation is accelerating". - WINDOW — how long the opening lasts, and what closes it. Ideal: a specific closing event with a date ("window closes at nCino go-live, Q2 2026"), because a window with no closing condition is not a window. - CONSEQUENCE OF WAITING — which assessed capability degrades, and by when. Ideal: named cell, direction, horizon. - COST OF ACTING NOW — the honest other side. Ideal: the concurrent commitment this would collide with, drawn from the timeline and the issue register — a live core migration, an open consent order, an integration already tracking to Q3. If acting now is genuinely cheap, say why, but say it. - WHY THIS SEQUENCE — why this is first rather than second, tied to the roadmap.
Second, the cost of acting now is not optional. A why-now that only lists upside is a pitch, and an AE who presents it to a client mid-core-conversion loses the room. The measured timeline for this client shows an acquisition "integration tracking to Q3 2026 · expected to reduce P2C1 score temporarily during cutover" — that is exactly the kind of fact that belongs under COST OF ACTING NOW.

Why this is what it is — Executive summary (SCQA) (4.4 O4)

An executive summary earns its place by doing four things the rest of the page cannot. The safeguard against score-recapping has to be mechanical, because prose drifts: no sentence may consist of a score predicate, at most one numeric score may appear in the whole summary, and the Complication must contain a causal connective. Why this is what it is — Insight cards (5.1 I1)
The review asked directly: what really IS an insight card, what makes it useful, and what are the non-negotiables. The answer the rest of this section is built on:
An insight card is a DEFENSIBLE ARGUMENT THAT CHANGES WHAT SOMEONE DOES. It has five parts — a claim, the evidence for it, the mechanism connecting them, a competing explanation considered and rejected, and the decision it implies. Remove any one and it stops being an insight.
Observation: "Onboarding scores 2.1." Insight: "Onboarding is the constraint on deposit growth, because applications abandon at identity verification, which is why the branch channel is absorbing volume the digital channel was built to take."
The observation gives a number the reader could have read off the heatmap. The insight gives a causal chain they could not. The reader's response to the first is "noted"; to the second, "then we fix IDV before we market the app".
What makes one useful to an AE: it survives being challenged. The client says "that's not right", the AE opens the drilldown, and the argument holds. What makes one useful to the client: it tells them something about themselves they had not assembled — usually because the evidence sat in two different departments.
The non-negotiables, all eight: - A claim, not a metric. what_text never opens with or consists of a score. - A mechanism. why_text explains HOW the claim produces the consequence. - A decision. so_what_text names what changes. "Consider investing in automation" is not a decision. - A live anchor. linked_subcap_id resolves to a cell this run serves; 15 of 119 findings had dead links. - Citations. The serve layer excludes uncited cards, so an uncited card is wasted work. - A rejected alternative. This is what separates analysis from assertion. - Severity by CONSEQUENCE, not by score distance. - Zero cards on a completed run is a failure state, not an empty state.
The best cards come from JOINING two sources that sit apart: a complaint theme against a process score, a job posting against a platform tenure, a regulator finding against a self-description. Look for those joins before writing anything.

11. Cross-cutting contracts
11.1 The analyst preamble (prepended to every prompt)
You are a Zennify DMA analyst preparing material an AE will take into a
client meeting. You are not a copywriter and not a scorer.

  - You EXTRACT and EXPLAIN. You never compute a score, never re-rank a
    platform fit, and never invent a figure.
  - Every claim about the client carries an E-ID. If you cannot cite it,
    you do not write it.
  - Where a source is absent, you say so and let the surface render an
    honest empty state. Absent beats wrong: a guessed field survives into
    a meeting and costs credibility.
  - You write in plain professional English. No consultant register
    ("leverage", "journey", "best-in-class"), no deficit framing
    ("lacks", "fails to"), no raw taxonomy codes (P2C3.1, M3, REC-04) in
    anything a client reads.
  - Numbers you quote must match the cell you name, at the grain you name
    it. This is the single most common defect class in this product.
  - Output is JSON matching the section contract. No prose outside it.

row of subcap_scores. If the score belongs to the category and the id to the
sub-capability, STOP and emit grain_violation - do not write prose over a
mismatch. This is the most common defect in this product: one line pairing a
sub-capability's score with a category's id produced 125 violations.

STEP 2 - EMIT THE EVIDENCE LIST FIRST, THE SYNTHESIS SECOND
Per item: {e_id, tier, claim_label, recency, source_title, publisher, excerpt}.
excerpt is verbatim, 50-500 chars, never a bare URL. Count them. That count is
what the synthesis may reason over, and it is printed beside the synthesis.

STEP 3 - WRITE THE SYNTHESIS  (40-90 words)
What the evidence establishes about THIS capability, where the score sits
against the peer median, and what follows for the client. One clause of
consequence: at or above median -> what to protect; below -> what it constrains
downstream. Cite inline. Do NOT open by restating the score - it is already
rendered above you.

STEP 4 - PEER FIGURE
If the peer table lacks a figure: recompute the median at lower N (floor 3),
then adjacency inference, then proxy ceiling, then stop. Label an inferred
figure INFERENCE with one clause of reasoning. Use the literal phrase
"peer proxy" when proxying. Never impute a value into the cell. Never write
"identical methodology". Uncertainty over +/-0.8 -> "Cannot reliably estimate".

 B  What would refute the synthesis? Run at least one contradictory query:
    "[Entity] [capability area] failure complaint outage criticism".
 D  Probes: grain mismatch; identity contamination (is every item about THIS
    entity, not a same-named one? check the domain on every source);
    staleness (a time-sensitive figure with no as-of date); tier

    Marketing-Reality Gap (T5 claims exceeding T1-T3 evidence).
 E  ACCEPT / REJECT / UNCERTAIN. REJECT -> emit the evidence list with
    synthesis=null and thin=true rather than prose you cannot defend.

Fewer than 3 items: run the ladder tiers 1-6 before settling for thin. Mint
E-CC ids with url + verbatim excerpt + retrieval date + tier + claim label. A
cell upgraded from thin to cited is the highest-value work on this surface. If
the ladder still yields nothing, emit thin=true and record what was searched -
searched-and-not-found is a finding; silence is not.

18.4 ERS renders below its own floor — RESOLVED: it is a defect

20. How the payload reaches the app
Every surface in this document is produced as JSON and handed to the app through one MCP transport. Nothing is written directly to a serving table, and nothing renders until it has passed validation and been promoted. This section is the mechanical half of the specification: what to send, where it goes, and what has to be true before an AE sees it.
The authority is app/mcp/contracts.py and app/mcp/router.py. Everything below is read out of that code rather than designed on paper, so a producing agent can call get_page_contract and receive the same shape this section describes.

20.3 The field shape
Each field is a tuple. The checker reads only the first three slots; the fourth travels to the producing agent.
{"field": (required?, type, item_type|None, doc?)}

  required?   True -> the section is invalid without it
  type        the python type the value must be
  item_type   applies when type is list — usually dict
  doc         the field's spec text, surfaced VERBATIM by
              get_page_contract

Nested section specs live under "_sections".

Why doc exists is worth repeating, because it is the difference between a contract and a shape. For a list[dict] field, {required, type, items} tells a producing agent nothing about the item keys — so the item shape lived only in the writers, the agent had to guess, and a guess that type-checks promotes silently wrong content. Sections whose items are objects now carry their item schema in doc, and it travels with the contract.
20.4 Submit → verdict → promote
1  get_report_bundle(run_id)              read the parsed package
2  get_page_contract(page)                read the shape, per page
3  produce the sections                   this document, per surface
4  register_evidence(...) per new source  BEFORE citing it; returns the e_id
5  submit_page_payload(run_id, page,      -> {submission_id, verdict}
                       payload, provenance)
6  repeat 3-5 until every page is PASS
7  promote_run(run_id)                    -> {promoted: true, stats}

What submit actually does, in order: it runs validate_submission for the page, then runs validate_evidence_section separately, because an extraction can only be checked against the run's own evidence store and the cited artifact's bytes — which is database work of a different shape from the register and format sweeps. ANY evidence reason at all fails the submission. The rationale in the code is worth quoting: an excerpt is either a copy of something a document says or it is not evidence. The check lives at submit rather than at promote so the producing agent can still repair the span.
Then it SUPERSEDES any live row for (run, page) — a resubmission replaces, it does not accumulate — and stages the payload into enrichment_staging with its status, its verdict, and submitted_by='claude-code'.
promote_run is all-or-nothing across the whole run. It requires a PASS row for EVERY page; anything else returns 409 with missing_pages and unpassed_pages. On success it runs WRITERS[page] per page into the serving tables and marks the staging rows promoted. There is no partial promote, so a run is never half-new and half-old on screen.

FIRST: O1 is cross-page. The Overview hero renders the pillar scores and peer deltas, but there is no overview.scores section — the figures come from heatmap.workbook_scores. SECOND, and more consequential: NINE of the 32 surfaces have no payload section at all — O1b, O10, O11, O12, C3, C5, P2b, H7 and H8. 20.7 Presentation rules the payload must satisfy
ORDER IS MEANING. A list renders in the order you send it. Findings are ranked, roadmap phases are sequenced, timeline events are chronological. Do not rely on the client to sort.
EMPTY IS A VALUE, NOT AN OMISSION. A surface with nothing to say sends its explicit empty state — verified_absent for leadership, verified_sparse for financial_series and timeline, empty_state for stairstep. A missing required section fails the contract; an honest empty state passes and renders correctly.
IDS ARE THE JOINS. subcap_id, e_id, rec_id and ic_id are what let the app wire a card to its drilldown. An id that does not resolve is a dead control, which is why the validator treats fabrication as fatal.
NO MARKDOWN IN A FIELD THAT RENDERS AS TEXT. story_md and scqa_md go through a stripper; everything else renders literally, so a stray '### ' reaches the AE verbatim. 127 clients shipped exactly that.
CITATIONS INLINE AS CHIPS, IDS BARE IN ARRAYS. [E-047] or [E-047, E-089] inside prose; bare E-047 inside e_ids[]. The brackets belong to the chip, not to the id.
TERMINAL PUNCTUATION on every prose field. 452 bodies across 136 clients shipped without it.
INTERNAL RUNGS MARKED. ERS, scoring rationale and synthetic provenance carry internal_only so the serve layer can strip them for the Customer audience. If the payload does not mark them, the app cannot hide them.

21.1 Exactly two places an id may come from
PACKAGE EVIDENCE. E-047, from evidence_index.csv. Already in the store with an entity, a run, a tier and a URL. You cite it; you never create it.
ENRICHMENT MINTS. Anything found outside the package is REGISTERED first and cited second, via register_evidence, which returns the id. The producer does not choose it — the server allocates E-CC-{seq:03d}, scoped per run.
Register before you cite. An id you invented and then used is fabricated by construction, even if a matching source exists.
The server computes ERS, not you: register_evidence calls compute_ers(tier, recency_tag, excerpt, facts).
Registration is idempotent by content — it dedups on content_hash = SHA256(url + claim_type + normalize(excerpt[:500])) scoped to the entity, and returns {e_id, deduped: true} when the source is already known. That is how one annual report is cited by six cards without producing six rows.
origin='claude-code' is stamped on mints, so enrichment stays separable from package evidence in audit and in gates.
Never invent, never guess, never renumber. Not E-999 as a placeholder, not E-001 because the excerpt looks like the first one, not renumbering to tidy a list. An id is a database key AND a click-through control: a wrong one is a broken control, which is worse than no chip because it looks like diligence.

Case-sensitive by design — lowercasing makes every “e-” in ordinary prose a candidate.
No partial matches: E-047abc is not E-047. The trailing (?![A-Za-z0-9-]) prevents a match ending mid-token where \b would not.
Order preserved, because the evidence-hygiene recovery path relies on the first token of a truncated comma-joined cell being the intact one.
app/mcp/formatting.EID_TOKEN_RE is the same recogniser on the MCP boundary, and tests/test_evidence_ids.py pins the two to each other — if they disagree, an id valid on one side of the wire is invalid on the other.
25 tests cover it, including one that asserts each of the five former call sites still shares the single pattern, so drifting back to a private regex fails the suite rather than re-opening the hole quietly.
21.3 The validator — V1 to V4, fail-closed
grounding_validator.validate_response runs on every generated surface. On ANY flag the surface fails closed: serve the template-fill fallback and write a gemini_hallucination_alerts row. An AE never sees unvalidated content.

Check
V1
cited_evidence_ids ⊆ the retrieved bundle — you may only cite what you were given
V2
no fabricated E- / subcap / IC- / REC- ids, by regex AND by DB existence
V3
no fabricated agent ids (AF-*, FM-AGENT-*) unless present in tech_stack_entries
V4
re-embed the response; require cosine ≥ 0.55 against the retrieved-bundle centroid

V4 is the one that catches what the others cannot. V1 to V3 are id checks, so a fluent paraphrase that invents a claim while citing only real ids passes all three. V4 asks whether the text is ABOUT the bundle at all. It runs on the local MiniLM tier and ABSTAINS when no embedding tier is available — an extra guard, never a fail-closed on a missing model.
ValidationFlags.is_clean is the gate and covers fabricated_e_ids, fabricated_subcap_ids, fabricated_ic_ids, fabricated_rec_ids, fabricated_agents and citation_set_mismatch. Note the last: citing a real id that was NOT in your bundle is a flag in its own right, because it means the reasoning reached outside its grounding.
21.4 The producer's own obligations, before submit
The validator is a backstop, not a plan — a surface that fails validation is thrown away and re-produced.
RESOLVE EVERY ID. Confirm it exists, belongs to THIS entity, and is linked to the run you are producing for. An id that resolves for a different entity puts another institution's data on this client's screen.
VERIFY EVERY EXCERPT against the fetched bytes. Verbatim, 50–500 chars, present in the document you actually fetched. Assert it byte-for-byte and drop the item if it does not match. Never a URL as an excerpt, never a summary presented as a quote. This is the check that separates “I read the source” from “I remember the source”.
CHECK THE SOURCE DOMAIN against the entity. The cheapest identity check available, and the one that would have caught the live contamination in §18.6: farmcrediteast.com as the source domain for a Utah bank is not a formatting question. Where the domain belongs to another institution, set identity_ok=false, quarantine, and do not let it count toward coverage (O10) or the tier distribution (O11).
DIFF YOUR OWN CITATIONS. find_fabricated(text, bundle_ids) must return empty before you submit. If it does not, you either mis-typed an id or reasoned past your evidence — both are fixed by returning to the bundle, never by deleting the citation and keeping the claim.
COUNT WHAT YOU CITE. Where a surface declares its grounding (“on the 1 item above”), that number is len(e_ids) and must be computed.
21.5 Attribution — the right id on the right cell
A real id cited on the wrong capability is the quietest of the four failures, because everything renders and nothing 404s. One measured case: a Forbes ranking linked to an Open-Banking sub-capability — a real source, a real cell, and no relationship between them. Corpus-wide, misattribution ran about 10× over budget, and 12 clients had 100% unlinked evidence, so every drawer on every cell rendered empty.
Link on SUBSTANCE, not proximity. An item supports a cell when it speaks to that capability, not when it was found while researching the category. The commonest cause of over-linking is one category-level search mapped identically onto five sub-capabilities.
A cell whose every drawer is empty is a LINKING failure, not an evidence gap. Diagnose before enriching: enriching a linker bug adds evidence that also will not render.
supports_subcap_ids[] is bidirectional in the UI — the evidence drawer renders “· supports:” chips that jump to the other cells an item backs, so a wrong link is visible from two directions.
21.6 Cross-entity leakage
Evidence is entity-scoped, but the dedup engine deliberately allows cross_entity_kept: the same public document can legitimately be evidence for two institutions — a regulator's industry report, a vendor's customer list. That makes the serving-side filter load-bearing.
Cohort mode 'single' may only pull ids owned by the current entity; the RAG re-rank enforces this when pulling in preferred ids from learning signals.
Cross-entity patterns (H8) emit counts and shares only. entity_ids[] is for internal audit and must be stripped from anything an AE or client sees.
dedup_audit records which branch fired per id — kept, dedup_same_entity, cross_entity_kept, duplicate_within_run, tier_upgrade — so a leak is traceable after the fact rather than mysterious.
21.7 When an id cannot be established
In order, stopping at the first that applies:
ENRICH. Run the ladder, find a real source, register it, cite the returned id. This is the answer most of the time and the highest-value work on any surface.
DROP THE CLAIM, KEEP THE SURFACE. If nothing supports the sentence, delete the sentence. A shorter honest card beats a complete unfounded one.
EMIT THE EMPTY STATE WITH WHAT YOU SEARCHED. Absence with a recorded search is a finding and earns an ERS. Absence with no record is a research failure wearing a finding's clothes.
NEVER keep the claim and remove the citation. An uncited claim is excluded by the serve layer anyway, so it is wasted work as well as wrong.
