D1 · Overview

Overview — surface contracts

The page an AE is guaranteed to read. It answers one question — what is true about this client, and what do I lead with — and it is the only client page with a customer variant that renders rather than locks. Thirteen surfaces, twelve sections, and five of them are confidence instrumentation.

O1 · Scores & peer benchmarks

Payload section overview.scores · component ScoreRing

Contract. Composite to 1dp with its band word, four pillar scores with peer deltas, posture chip, evidence basis, and an 18–32 word framing sentence that states the gap, quantifies it and localises it.

What must be presented

  • Overall maturity to 1dp in the hero ring, with its band word (Building, Competitive…).
  • Four pillar scores P1–P4 as a bar strip, each to 1dp with peer delta arrow.
  • Peer median per pillar and the cohort the median is drawn from.
  • Every number must equal the served cell to within one 2dp rounding step. The hero ring and the run row must render the SAME number — they are two views of one value.

Why it is shaped this way

The hero is the only card an AE is guaranteed to read, so it carries the whole argument in one screen: the composite, the four pillar scores with their peer deltas, the posture label, and — measured on this render — a FIRMOGRAPHICS strip. For the client it must be recognisably them; for the AE it must supply the one number that frames the meeting and the named capability behind it.

What the render establishes: the composite is 3.4, the posture chip reads COMPETING, provenance reads EVIDENCE · HYBRID, and the framing line is "Trails regional bank peer median by -0.3 points. Gap concentrated in P4 Data foundation." That last sentence is the model for this card. It is not a score read-out — it states the gap, quantifies it, and localises it. A hero that says "overall maturity 3.4" has told the AE nothing they cannot see.

Peer scores are frequently absent, and this is where fabrication happens. The sanctioned answers, in order, are: recompute the median at a lower N by dropping the peer that lacks the figure (floor N=3; N=5 -> sorted[2], N=4 -> mean of sorted[1..2], N=3 -> sorted[1]); then adjacency inference from a neighbouring sub-capability, labelled INFERENCE with its reasoning; then a proxy ceiling (a LinkedIn specialist ratio under 5% caps P1C4 at L2.5, negative-dominant Glassdoor caps at L3.0, lowest cap wins); then stop and print "Cannot reliably estimate". Never impute a value into the cell. The report must disclose proxying with the literal phrase "peer proxy", because a governance check greps for it, and must never claim "identical methodology".

The grain rule owns this card. A quoted number and the cell id it names must be the same cell. One line that paired a sub-capability's score with a category's id produced 125 gate violations across the corpus; it fell to 40, then 8, then 1 as each layer was fixed and re-measured, and none of it was accommodated by raising a ceiling.

STEP 5 - POSTURE LEADING │ COMPETING │ LAGGING │ MIXED, justified against the peer set, with the evidence basis chip (EVIDENCE / HYBRID / INFERRED) set from what actually backs the scores. C Is the posture reasonable for this sub-vertical, size tier and regulator? D Probes: grain mismatch; identity contamination (every figure must be THIS entity - check the domain on every source); Peer Outlier (a capability wildly unlike peers needs its evidence re-verified); staleness. O2 · Firmographics strip

Payload section overview.firmographics · component ScoreRing inline strip

Contract. Assets, employees and branches rendered inside the hero rather than as a separate panel. Every figure identity-checked against the entity.

What must be presented

  • Employees, revenue, AUM or assets, CAGR, HQ, branches, founded year, primary regulator, charter — each as value + provenance.
  • Every populated field shows where it came from; an unknown field renders an em dash, never a guess and never a dict repr.

O3 · Why-now signals

Payload section overview.why_now · component WhyNowStrip

Contract. Dated, cited events. Each carries trigger, window, consequence of waiting, cost of acting now and why this sequence.

What must be presented

  • Three to six trigger cards, each a DATED external event with a kind pill, a one-line body and its E-ID.
  • Each signal answers 'why call them now' — a leadership change, an earnings move, a regulatory action, a technology announcement, an M&A event.
  • A signal is an EVENT, not a score read-out. 'P2 scores 2.4' is not a why-now.
  • No signal may be the assessment itself ('Zennify completed a Digital Maturity Assessment' shipped on 11 clients and is circular).

Why it is shaped this way

A why-now is not a list of changes. It is the synthesis of the whole report, answering one question: why should this entity act NOW rather than next year. Changes are the raw material; the synthesis is the product.

Measured on this render the card is "Why now signals · 3 triggers · click any signal to drill into the evidence", and the drilldown returns reasoning of the form "Closing P1C1.6.4 lifts the parent category and unblocks downstream work." That is the right shape — a trigger tied to a cell, tied to a consequence.

Two things the review identified as missing, both of which are now required.

First, the card must be SEGMENTED, and each header has an ideal: - TRIGGER — what changed, dated, cited. Ideal: a dated external event with a regulator, a filing, a vendor milestone or a named hire behind it. Not "digital transformation is accelerating". - WINDOW — how long the opening lasts, and what closes it. Ideal: a specific closing event with a date ("window closes at nCino go-live, Q2 2026"), because a window with no closing condition is not a window. - CONSEQUENCE OF WAITING — which assessed capability degrades, and by when. Ideal: named cell, direction, horizon. - COST OF ACTING NOW — the honest other side. Ideal: the concurrent commitment this would collide with, drawn from the timeline and the issue register — a live core migration, an open consent order, an integration already tracking to Q3. If acting now is genuinely cheap, say why, but say it. - WHY THIS SEQUENCE — why this is first rather than second, tied to the roadmap.

O4 · Executive summary

Payload section overview.exec_summary · component SCQACard

Contract. Situation, complication, question, answer. At most one raw score, and client facts must outnumber score references.

What must be presented

  • A Situation–Complication–Question–Answer narrative, 4 short paragraphs, that an AE can read aloud.
  • At most ONE raw score in the whole summary; the story carries the argument, not the arithmetic. 131/138 bodies once quoted two or more.
  • Every factual claim carries an E-ID. Client facts must outnumber score references.
  • Any score quoted must resolve to a served cell under the label used — quoting a category average under a subcapability's name is the O1/S23 grain defect.

Why it is shaped this way

Measured, this card is "Executive narrative · SCQA · Assessment Report · stored verbatim · Read full ↓". The review's objection stands: a summary that recaps scores is not a summary. The scores are already on the same screen, twice.

An executive summary earns its place by doing four things the rest of the page cannot. It synthesises the CORPUS rather than the scorecard — the report, the evidence, the issue register, the sentiment, the stack. It is DATA-LED, opening on the fact that most changes the reader's mind. It names the RECOMMENDATIONS and, critically, JUSTIFIES THEIR SEQUENCING, because sequencing is the one judgement the client cannot make from the heatmap. And it is one story that survives contact with the other five pages.

SCQA is the structure, and each element has a job: - SITUATION — the client's own position in their own terms, recognisable to them, with a figure that is theirs. - COMPLICATION — the constraint. This is where the analysis lives. It is a mechanism, not a low score: "applications abandon at identity verification, so the branch channel is absorbing volume the digital channel was built to take" is a complication; "onboarding scores 2.1" is a measurement. - QUESTION — the decision the client faces, phrased as they would phrase it. - ANSWER — the sequenced recommendation, with the reason the sequence is that way, and what it costs to wait.

The safeguard against score-recapping has to be mechanical, because prose drifts: no sentence may consist of a score predicate, at most one numeric score may appear in the whole summary, and the Complication must contain a causal connective. A corpus sweep found 219 findings across 83 clients opening with "stands at 2.34/5", which is what happens without a mechanical rule.

O5 · Opportunity surface tiles

Payload section overview.opportunity · component OpportunitySurfaceStrip

Contract. Where engagement value concentrates per platform, tied to the cells below M3 that the platform addresses.

What must be presented

  • Five tiles naming where value is available, each with a headline, a one-line rationale and its anchor capability.
  • Prose must be whole sentences — tiles once shipped head-clipped mid-word.
  • Each tile names a capability in words and ties to a platform the client could use.

Why it is shaped this way

Measured: "Opportunity Surface · per platform · Composite fit score 0-100 · Σ(priority × gap) for ABS", and clicking a tile NAVIGATES to #/clients/<id>/platform?platform=DB — it is a cross-page link into the Platform page scoped to that platform, not a panel.

The review's objection is that these tiles are shallow: a composite number with no visible reasoning and no validation. A fit score is a claim about what the client should buy. It is the most commercially loaded number in the product and it must be the most defensible.

Three requirements follow. The composite must be DECOMPOSABLE — the tile shows Σ(priority × gap) and the drilldown must reproduce exactly that arithmetic from the cells it names. A tile showing 47/100 whose expansion recomputes to something else is the defect class that hit 570 of 685 cards; the fix is to rebake the factors after any override rather than render two numbers from two code paths.

It must be VALIDATED against the assessment report, not just the score matrix. The report says what the analyst concluded; if the arithmetic ranks a platform the report never discusses, that disagreement is a finding about one of them.

O6 · Top findings

Payload section overview.findings · component TopFindingsCard

Contract. Ranked findings, each with what, why and so what, a named consequence, a rejected alternative, and an inline expansion.

What must be presented

  • Four to six findings, each a capability-named headline with a What/Why/So-what body and evidence chips.
  • The score chip and the anchor id must name the SAME cell. A subcap-grain score under a category id read '3.5/5' against a cell serving 2.77 on 59 clients.
  • The title must be a capability, not an evidence sentence, not a person's name, not a raw code, and not the body restated.
  • Bodies end in terminal punctuation and never repeat a sentence.

Why it is shaped this way

The render is more structured than the earlier specification allowed for, and the structure is the answer to "what makes a compelling finding". Measured, each row is: an id chip (F-01), a title that is a CLAIM, a THEME label, and a QUANTIFIED CONSEQUENCE — plus platform chips and a chevron:

F-02 "Loan origination is where automation lands first"

F-03 "The team generates insight faster than it acts on it"

Contract. Triage layout grouped by priority, pillar or theme. Each card carries a claim as its title and opens a four-tab modal.

What must be presented

Why it is shaped this way

The review asked directly: what really IS an insight card, what makes it useful, and what are the non-negotiables. The answer the rest of this section is built on:

An insight card is a DEFENSIBLE ARGUMENT THAT CHANGES WHAT SOMEONE DOES. It has five parts — a claim, the evidence for it, the mechanism connecting them, a competing explanation considered and rejected, and the decision it implies. Remove any one and it stops being an insight.

Observation: "Onboarding scores 2.1." Insight: "Onboarding is the constraint on deposit growth, because applications abandon at identity verification, which is why the branch channel is absorbing volume the digital channel was built to take."

The observation gives a number the reader could have read off the heatmap. The insight gives a causal chain they could not. The reader's response to the first is "noted"; to the second, "then we fix IDV before we market the app".

Why it is shaped this way

Safeguard gates are the client-visible expression of our own discipline, which makes honesty here structural rather than optional. A gate that reports PASS because it did not run is worse than a gate that reports FAIL.

Each gate states what it checked, what it found, and what a failure would mean for the reader's confidence in the surrounding numbers. The three classes behave differently and must be labelled as such: BLOCK gates (identity, regulatory anchor, traceability, classification anchors, no toolkit blending) stop publication; CONFIDENCE gates reduce the confidence band without stopping it; LABELLING gates force a relabel - a trend with fewer than three dated points becomes a snapshot rather than disappearing.

Page operation

How each page operates

Every page follows the same skeleton — resolve, fetch, redact, render, interact — and each diverges from it in ways that matter. Below is the operational sequence for all seven dashboards.

The shared invariants

Every page is server-rendered from serving tables in a single round trip. Every drilldown payload ships inline on that response, so no panel fetches. Redaction happens at serialisation, so the browser never receives an internal field. Ordering is payload ordering. And no page computes a colour, a score, a delta or an age — all four are either promoted or generated by the database.

Four independent version axes. Conflating any two of them produces a comparison that looks valid and is not.

Run version

One complete assessment of one entity at one point in time.

Promotion swaps which run is ACTIVE. Prior runs stay queryable forever — the longitudinal surfaces read across them.

Catalogue version

Which capability catalogue the run was scored against.

Pinned at ingest and never changed for that run. Cross-run comparison is only valid within a lineage; across a bump every cell resolves through the alias bridge or is marked not-comparable.

Producer version

Which synthesis produced each section.

Stamped on every serving row. Lets one page be re-synthesised and re-promoted without re-running the assessment, and lets a regression be bisected to a producer.

Contract version

The payload shape the submission validated against.

Recorded on the staged row. A contract bump invalidates staged-but-unpromoted work for the changed sections only; promoted content is unaffected until re-synthesised.

What a version diff may and may not assert

ComparisonPermitted
Two runs, same catalogue versionCell-by-cell. Score deltas, coverage deltas, evidence-count deltas, recommendation carry-over.
Two runs, catalogue bump, cell bridged by an aliasCell-by-cell, with the rename disclosed. The diff names both ids.
Two runs, catalogue bump, cell split or mergedCategory level only. The cell row renders NOT_COMPARABLE with the reason.
Two runs, catalogue bump, cell retiredNot comparable. Rendered as retired, never as a drop to zero.
Composite across a catalogue bumpDisclosed as approximate. The denominator changed, so the composite is not strictly the same measurement.
Any comparison across entitiesNever on a client surface. Cross-entity work is counts and shares only, on Health.
The failure this prevents

A renamed cell compared naively across a catalogue bump reads as a capability that vanished — which looks like a regression, gets investigated as one, and is actually a rename. A split cell compared naively reads as a score that halved. Both are the diff asserting more than the data supports, and both are prevented by refusing the comparison rather than by annotating it afterwards.

Re-synthesising one page without a new run

A section can be wrong without the assessment being wrong. The producer version axis exists so that case does not require a rerun.

1 The run is already PROMOTED. Its staging rows are retained, marked promoted. 2 A Cowork session re-claims the run. 3 It resubmits ONE page. That supersedes the live staging row for that page and carries a new producer_version. 4 promote_run runs again. It asserts a PASS on every page — five come from the retained staging rows, one from the new submission — and rewrites all 34 serving tables in one transaction. 5 Rows from the five unchanged pages are rewritten byte-identically but with a new promoted_at. Rows from the changed page carry the new producer_version. The atomic guarantee is unaffected: promotion is still all-or-nothing across all 6 pages. What changes is that four of them did not need re-synthesising, only re-promoting.

This is why promoted staging rows are retained rather than deleted. If promotion cleared its inputs, fixing one card would mean re-synthesising all 6 pages — which in practice means nobody fixes one card.

Content

Global — identical for every user · Promoted section rows, evidence, scores, gates

Never per-user. Two AEs opening the same client see the same numbers. Only the audience filter differs, and audience is a request parameter, not a user attribute.

Synthesis session

Owned by one Cowork session at a time · The claim lease, staged submissions, verdicts

Staged work outlives the session. The lease expires so a dead session cannot block a run; a new session resumes from run progress.

User workspace

Per user, per entity · Annotations, alert dispositions, last-viewed run, saved searches

Survives sign-out. Never presents as synthesis output — an annotation is attributed and dated.

View state

Per user, per tab — ephemeral but shareable · Audience, heatmap view mode and grain, open panel, filters, Health tab

Nothing server-side. A URL is the unit of sharing: paste it to a colleague and they see exactly your view, subject to their own role.

Pinned run

Per user, per entity · Which run the user is currently reading

Set on first load so a promotion mid-session cannot silently swap the numbers under an open page.

Two AEs, one client

AE-1 opens /clients/shore-united-bank/overview AE-2 opens /clients/shore-united-bank/overview same moment Both resolve to run 3 (ACTIVE) and read the same 12 serving rows. Both see IDENTICAL numbers, prose and citations. What can differ: · audience a URL parameter, not a user attribute · pinned run AE-1 may still be reading run 2 from a shared link · view state grain, filters, open panel — all in the URL · annotations each sees their own AND their colleagues', attributed · role an AE and an analyst see different tab sets Content is never per-user. There is no per-user copy of a payload, no per-user cache, and nothing to reconcile between two readers.

A promotion lands while a page is open

t0 AE opens /clients/x/overview → resolves run 2, pins ?run=<run2> t1 Cowork promotes run 3 → run 2 becomes SUPERSEDED t2 AE clicks the Heatmap tab → still ?run=<run2>, still run 2 t3 A banner appears: "A newer assessment is available." t4 AE clicks through → ?run=<run3>, whole shell reloads The numbers never change under an open page. An AE who quoted a figure at t0 can still see that figure at t2. Silent mid-session substitution is the one behaviour this design refuses outright.
Why pinning is not optional

An AE reads the hero, walks into a meeting, and refers back to the tab twenty minutes later. If the run swapped in between, the figure they quoted is no longer on screen and they cannot tell whether they misread it or the data moved. Pinning costs one URL parameter and removes the entire class.

A Cowork session dies mid-synthesis

t0 session A claims run 3, lease expires at t0+2h t1 session A submits overview → PASS, STAGED t2 session A submits insights → FAIL, 3 reasons t3 session A dies Staged work persists. The overview submission is untouched. The verdict persists. Including the failing one. t4 lease expires. The run returns to unclaimed. t5 session B claims run 3 and calls get_run_progress: overview pass insights fail (3 reasons) heatmap missing platform missing context missing techstack missing promotable: false t6 session B reads the three reasons, repairs, resubmits insights. It never re-synthesises the overview.

Retention

Deliberately an operational policy rather than a contract. The one constraint the contract does impose: a superseded run must remain queryable long enough for the longitudinal surfaces — velocity, recurring themes, the seen-in-N-runs chip and version diff — to read across it. In practice that means every run for an entity that is still an active account.

Card anatomy and colour

Every surface renders into a fixed shell. Prose written without knowing where it lands is prose that clips mid-word — a measured defect on 501 cards.

Three conflicts worth knowing before writing a band word

Transformational does not render — the resolver has four branches and anything at or above 4.0 returns Differentiating. The M2 hex disagrees between sources — #62D7B8 in the resolver, #B0EDD3 in the published palette; the resolver wins because it is what renders. And the word "stale" carries two meanings — over 12 months on the freshness dot, 36 to 48 months on the evidence ladder. The evidence ladder governs anything the payload emits.

Describe the number, not the colour. "Data foundation sits at 1.9" is checkable. "Data foundation is red" depends on a palette the writer does not control and cannot see. Thin evidence adds a dashed outline and does not change the fill, because the fill means maturity and nothing else.
